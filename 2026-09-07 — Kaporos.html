מִנְהַג הַכַּפָּרוֹת — בֵּין הָרַשְׁבָּ"א וְהַמַּהֲרִי"ל
Kaporos — The Custom the Rashba Tried to Abolish
A Shiur by Rabbi Yisroel Kraz · Erev Yom Kippur 5787
Recorded: Elul 5786 · Prepared: 26 Elul 5786 / September 7, 2026

Few pre-Yom Kippur customs are as fiercely contested — and as tenaciously preserved — as Kaporos. The Geonim already knew it; the Rashba and the Ramban wanted it abolished as דַּרְכֵי הָאֱמוֹרִי (bordering on idolatry); the Bais Yosef followed them and told his readers to put a stop to the custom; and yet the Rema, in a single glancing gloss, restored it and made it universal Ashkenaz practice. This shiur traces the fault-line from the Teshuvos HaGeonim through the Rashba, the Ramban, the Beis Yosef, the Maharil, the Mordechai, the Rema, and finally to the acharonim who reframed the entire question — and to the modern reservations of poskim watching what happens on the shechitah floor of an erev Yom Kippur.

I. A Custom the Geonim Already Knew

The Bais Yosef in Orach Chaim 605 opens by acknowledging that the custom of Kaporos — slaughtering a chicken (תַּרְנְגוֹל) on erev Yom Kippur as an expiation over one's head — is very old. It appears in a teshuvah of the Geonim, and the Geonim themselves seem to have received it as an established practice whose reason they did not know.1

יֵשׁ מְקוֹמוֹת שֶׁנּוֹהֲגִין לִשְׁחוֹט תַּרְנְגוֹל לְכַפָּרָה. וְכֵן יֵשׁ בִּתְשׁוּבַת הַגְּאוֹנִים שֶׁשְּׁאָלַתּוֹ שֶׁאָנוּ נוֹהֲגִין לִשְׁחוֹט עֶרֶב יוֹם הַכִּפּוּרִים תַּרְנְגוֹל וְאֵין אָנוּ יוֹדְעִין מִנְהָג זֶה לָמָּה, מַאי שְׁנָא תַּרְנְגוֹל מִבְּהֵמָה וְחַיָּה. הָא וַדַּאי קֻשְׁיָא הִיא. אֶלָּא שֶׁיֵּשׁ בָּהּ שְׁנֵי טְעָמִים: אֶחָד שֶׁהַתַּרְנְגוֹל מָצוּי בַּבַּיִת יוֹתֵר מִכָּל בְּהֵמָה חַיָּה וָעוֹף. וְעוֹד יֵשׁ בִּמְקוֹמוֹת שֶׁעוֹשִׂין עֲשִׁירִים תְּמוּרָה אֵילִים, וְעִיקָר בַּעֲלֵי קַרְנַיִם דְּמוּת אֵילוֹ שֶׁל יִצְחָק אָבִינוּ עָלָיו הַשָּׁלוֹם. לְפִיכָךְ לֹא דָּבָר קָבוּעַ הוּא. וְעוֹד שָׁמַעְנוּ מֵהַחֲכָמִים הָרִאשׁוֹנִים שֶׁאַף עַל פִּי שֶׁבְּהֵמָה דָּמֶיהָ יְקָרִים יוֹתֵר מִתַּרְנְגוֹל, אַף עַל פִּי כֵן תַּרְנְגוֹל מֻבְחָר. לְפִי שֶׁשְּׁמוֹ גֶּבֶר, כִּדְאָמְרִינַן (יוֹמָא כ:) מַאי קָרָא גֶבֶר? אָמַר רַב שִׁילָא קָרָא תַּרְנְגוֹלָא. וְכֵיוָן שֶׁשְּׁמוֹ גֶּבֶר, תְּמוּרַת גֶּבֶר בְּגֶבֶר תַּפִּי מֵהַנֵּי וּמֵעָלֵי. וְכָךְ רְגִילִין כָּאן: אוֹחֵז שְׁלִיחַ צִבּוּר הַתַּרְנְגוֹל וּמַנִּיחַ יָדוֹ עַל רֹאשׁוֹ וְנוֹטְלוֹ. וּמַנִּיחַ עַל רֹאשׁ הַמִּתְכַּפֵּר וְאוֹמֵר: זֶה תַּחַת זֶה, זֶה חִלּוּפוֹ שֶׁל זֶה, זֶה מְחוּלָּל עַל זֶה. — Bais Yosef, Orach Chaim 605, quoting Teshuvos HaGeonim  1

Three reasons are given for choosing specifically a chicken. First, it is the most commonly available animal in the household. Second, in some places wealthy men used a ram — אֵילִים — recalling eilo shel Yitzchak at the akeidah; a chicken is simply the modest man's equivalent. Third, and most beautifully — because the Gemara in Yoma 20b records that the word gever means both man and rooster: מַאי קָרָא גֶּבֶר? אָמַר רַב שִׁילָא קָרָא תַּרְנְגוֹלָא. A gever in place of a gever is the fitting temurah.2

II. The Rashba Wants It Stopped

Against this ancient practice stands the sharp voice of the Rashba (Rabbeinu Shlomo ben Aderes of Barcelona, d. 1310), in a teshuvah cited by the Beis Yosef and preserved in Teshuvos HaRashba siman 395. Someone had written to the Rashba describing the custom performed for na'arim — children — on erev Yom Kippur, and had added that he was inclined to say the shechitah of a Kaporos chicken is halachically invalid, k'mo shochet l'shem etamo: as if slaughtering for one's own bones, i.e., for an idolatrous or magical purpose.3

כָּתַב מָר כִּי רָאִיתָ מַה שֶּׁהִשְׁגַּתִּי אֲנִי בְּעִנְיַן הַכַּפָּרָה שֶׁעוֹשִׂין לַנְּעָרִים בְּעַרְבֵי יוֹם הַכִּפּוּרִים, וְשֶׁאָמַרְתִּי אֲנִי וְהִנַּאֲךָ. וְאַתָּה מוֹסִיף עוֹד לוֹמַר שֶׁקָּרוֹב לוֹמַר שֶׁהַשְּׁחִיטָה שֶׁלָּזֶה פְּסוּלָה כְּמוֹ שׁוֹחֵט לְשֵׁם עַצְמוֹתָיו. אֲנִי מְלֵאתִי מִנְהָג זֶה פָּשׁוּט בְּעִירֵנוּ עִם שְׁאָר דְּבָרִים שֶׁהָיוּ נוֹהֲגִין כְּיוֹצֵא בָּזֶה, שֶׁהָיוּ שׁוֹחֲטִין תַּרְנְגוֹל זָקֵן לְכַפָּרָה עַל הַנַּעַר הַיִּלּוֹד, וְתוֹלִין רֹאשׁוֹ וּמוֹתָלִין הָרֹאשׁ בְּנוּטָלוֹ בְּפֶתַח הַבַּיִת עִם שׁוּמִים, וְהַבְּלִים הַרְבֵּה שֶׁנִּרְאוּ בְּעֵינַי כְּדַרְכֵי הָאֱמוֹרִי. וְדָחַקְתִּי עַל זֶה הַרְבֵּה, וּמַחְמַד וְחֶסֶד עָלֵינוּ נִשְׁמְעוּ דְּבָרַי, וְלֹא נִשְׁאַר מִכָּל זֶה וּמִכַּיּוֹצֵא בּוֹ בְּעִירֵנוּ מְאוּמָה. — Teshuvos HaRashba, siman 395  3

The Rashba goes further and identifies the practice with דַּרְכֵי הָאֱמוֹרִי — "the ways of the Emorites" — the Torah's category for pagan, superstitious, quasi-magical practices. He writes that he was full of protest against this custom in his own city (Barcelona), and Hashem — chesed aleinu — caused his words to be heard, and nothing was left of it in our city.3

Nor was the Rashba alone. The Ramban (Rabbeinu Moshe ben Nachman) is cited by the Beis Yosef as having written the same thing in his Toras HaAdam, and the Beis Yosef adds that the Rashbatz (Rabbi Shimon ben Tzemach Duran) records the same objection in Tashbatz Katan siman 125.4

III. The Bais Yosef Rules — and the Rema Reverses Him

The Bais Yosef, faithful to his rishonim, concludes decisively:

מַה שֶּׁנּוֹהֲגִים לַעֲשׂוֹת כַּפָּרָה בְּעֶרֶב יוֹם כִּפּוּר לִשְׁחוֹט תַּרְנְגוֹל עַל כָּל בֵּן זָכָר וְלוֹמַר עָלָיו פְּסוּקִים — יֵשׁ לִמְנוֹעַ הַמִּנְהָג. — Bais Yosef, Orach Chaim 605  5

He instructs the reader to abolish the custom. And in the Shulchan Aruch itself (OC 605:1) he codifies it: מַה שֶּׁנּוֹהֲגִין לַעֲשׂוֹת כַּפָּרָה בְּעֶרֶב יוֹם כִּפּוּר לִשְׁחוֹט תַּרְנְגוֹל… יֵשׁ לִמְנוֹעַ הַמִּנְהָג.

And then comes the Rema, in a single terse gloss:

וְיֵשׁ מִן הַגְּאוֹנִים שֶׁכָּתְבוּ הַמִּנְהָג. וְכֵן כָּתְבוּ מִקְצַת הָאַחֲרוֹנִים. וְכֵן נָהֲגוּ בְּכָל מְדִינוֹת אֵלּוּ, וְאֵין לְשַׁנּוֹת כִּי הוּא מִנְהַג וָתִיקִין. — Rema, Shulchan Aruch OC 605:1  6

With those words the Rema flatly reverses the Bais Yosef. He acknowledges the Geonim who preserved the custom, the acharonim who wrote it up (Maharil chief among them), and declares it minhag vasikin — an ancient, pious practice, not to be disturbed. For Ashkenaz, this settled the matter.6

III½. The Maharil — Ashkenaz Recovers the Custom

The Rema's ruling rests on the Maharil (Rabbi Yaakov Moellin, d. 1427), whose sefer minhagim — the founding document of Ashkenaz halachic practice — records Kaporos in vivid detail. The Maharil holds that the concern of דַּרְכֵי הָאֱמוֹרִי is minimal here, because Klal Yisrael has been performing this custom for generations le'shem Shamayim, with piyutim and pesukim recited over the animal.7

כָּתַב מַהֲרִי"ל: דָּבָר וַדַּאי יֵשׁ לָחוּשׁ קְצָת לְדַרְכֵי הָאֱמוֹרִי כַּסִּמָּן הָאֱמוֹרִי, וְשׁוֹאֵל אָמַר מַהֲרִי"ל: אַף וֵאלַי מַהֲרִי"ל לֹא נָטַל לָבָן אֶלָּא כְּשֶׁלֹּא הָלַךְ לִשְׁאוֹל אַחֲרָיו, כְּגוֹן שֶׁמָּכְרוּ לוֹ תַּרְנְגוֹל לָבָן, אוֹ בָּקַנָּה תַּרְנְגוֹל בֵּין הַתַּרְנְגוֹלִים וְכַדַּלְמָא בְּפֶרֶק קַמָּא דַּעֲבוֹדָה זָרָה (יב:). אֲבָל לְדַקְדֵּק וְלִשְׁאוֹל אַחֲרָיו וּלְקַנּוֹתוֹ בְּדָמִים יְקָרִים — נִרְאָה מָרְדֳּכַי הָאֱמוֹרִי, וְכֵן קִבַּלְתִּי מֵאֲדוֹנִי אָבִי מוֹרִי ז"ל. — Maharil, Hilchos Erev Yom Kippur  7

The Maharil's own qualifier is important. Even he concedes that seeking out a white chicken specifically and paying premium for it edges over into דַּרְכֵי הָאֱמוֹרִי — because that is precisely the pagan practice condemned in Avodah Zarah 12b. But taking whatever chicken comes to hand, even if it happens to be white, is entirely in order.8

IV. A Chicken Is Not a Korban — the Maharil's Defense

The deeper of the Maharil's arguments is that we are not offering a korban. If someone were slaughtering the chicken l'shem korban outside the Beis HaMikdash, that would be shochet kadashim ba'chutz — a capital offense — and worse still, the Rashba's colleague was inclined to say the shechitah itself is pesulah, as if for one's own idolatrous ends.9

וְיֵשׁ אוֹמְרִים שֶׁיֵּשׁ לִמְנוֹעַ הַמִּנְהָג הַזֶּה וְאוֹמְרִים שֶׁאַדְּרַבָּה אִסּוּר יֵשׁ בַּדָּבָר, וְיֵשׁ בּוֹ מִשּׁוּם דַּרְכֵי הָאֱמוֹרִי, דְּאִי מִשּׁוּם קָרְבָּן הֲרֵי תַּרְנְגוֹל אָסוּר לְקָרְבָּן, וְעוֹד שֶׁהֲרֵי שׁוֹחֲטוֹ בַּחוּץ וְנִרְאֶה כְּשׁוֹחֵט קָדָשִׁים בַּחוּץ. וַאֲנִי אוֹמֵר אַדְּרַבָּה וְהִיא הַנּוֹתֶנֶת, דְּמִשּׁוּם זֶה נָהֲגוּ לִיקַּח מִינִים שֶׁאֵינָם רְאוּיִים לְקָרְבָּן, כְּדֵי שֶׁלֹּא יִטְעוּ לוֹמַר קָרְבָּן מַמָּשׁ הוּא וִיבוֹאוּ לְהַקְדִּישׁוֹ קְדוּשַׁת הַגּוּף מַמָּשׁ, וְיֹאכְלוּ מִשְּׁחִיטָתוֹ בַּחוּץ. לְכָךְ לוֹקְחִים מִינִים הַלָּלוּ שֶׁיָּדְעוּ הַכֹּל שֶׁלֹּא לְשֵׁם קָרְבָּן הִנְהִיגוּהוּ, אֶלָּא כְּדֵי לְהַשְׁבִּיעַ עֵינוֹ שֶׁל רָשָׁע. — Cited in Bais Yosef 605 in defense of the custom  9

Precisely because a chicken is not a valid korban, the defender argues, the people can never mistake this act for a real offering. The whole point of choosing a species eino ra'ui l'korban is to render the ceremony obviously non-sacrificial and yet spiritually potent — a "distraction" for the yetzer hara, an act that appeases the eye of the evildoer, so that a satan (שָׂטָן, יֵצֶר הָרָע, מַלְאַךְ הַמָּוֶת — all one) will "see" something and slacken his prosecution.9

V. Temurah — The Chicken as Substitute

The theological heart of Kaporos is the concept of temurah. The Kaporos ceremony imitates, in a diminished form, the halachic mechanism of a korban being substituted (תְּמוּרָה): the animal takes the place of the person, and what would have befallen the person befalls the animal instead.10

זֶה חֲלִיפָתִי, זֶה תְּמוּרָתִי, זֶה כַּפָּרָתִי. זֶה הַתַּרְנְגוֹל יֵלֵךְ לְמִיתָה וַאֲנִי אֵלֵךְ וְאֶכָּנֵס לְחַיִּים טוֹבִים אֲרוּכִּים וּלְשָׁלוֹם. — The formula recited during Kaporos  10

The Aruch and later Rishonim illustrate the concept with a story from the Gemara. When the Roman decree of destruction was rolling toward a certain city, Rabbi Akiva was in danger of being captured. In the parallel elsewhere, a rooster crowed at the wrong moment and the trap sprung on it instead. The Aruch (siman gever) preserves that image: the chicken takes the blow that was aimed at the man.11

כְּשֶׁכַּאֲשֶׁר בָּא הָרַ"ע לְאוֹתָהּ עִיר, הָיָה רָאוּי לָבֹא עָלָיו וְעַל כָּל הָעִיר, דְּהַיְינוּ שֶׁיִּהְיֶה נִשְׁבָּה כְּמוֹ שֶׁהָיוּ נִשְׁבִּים שְׁאָר הָעִיר. וְאִם הָיָה נִשְׁבָּה הָיוּ מְבַטְלִים אוֹתוֹ מִכָּל וְהָיוּ נוֹטְלִים מִמֶּנּוּ גּוּפוֹ וְנַפְשׁוֹ. וְהַשֵּׁם יִתְבָּרַךְ רָצָה לְהַצִּיל אֶת רַבִּי עֲקִיבָא, סָבַב דְּלָא יְהִיב לֵיהּ אוּשְׁפִּיזָא. וּלְפִיכָךְ אָתָא אַרְיֵה וְאָכַל הַחֲמוֹר וְדָבָר זֶה הוּא תְּמוּרָה מַה שֶּׁרָאוּי לָבֹא עַל גּוּפוֹ, וְאָכִיל הַתַּרְנְגוֹל וְדָבָר זֶה הוּא תְּמוּרָה נַפְשׁוֹ וְכַפָּרָתוֹ, וְהַנֵּר תְּמוּרַת הַשֵּׂכֶל עַד שֶׁיִּהְיֶה לוֹ תְּמוּרָה לְגַמְרֵי. — The Aruch on gever, and cited by later poskim  11

The Kaporos ceremony is thus not magic and not korban — but a middle path. It is a physical enactment of teshuvah: I look at an animal that has been slaughtered tachtai, in my place, and I am shaken into an awareness of my own mortality and my dependence on Hashem's mercy.12

VI. The Rashba's Halachic Weight — Even Against Everyone

The Beis Yosef in his teshuvah preserves a stunning line about the Rashba's authority: even if all the chachmei Yisrael of a generation were on one side of a scale and the Rashba were alone on the other, the Rashba would tip the scale for all of them.13

דְּלֹא שָׁבְקִינַן הָרַשְׁבָּ"א וְנָקְטִינַן כְּהָרִיבָ"שׁ, מִשּׁוּם דְּקִיַ"ל הֲלָכָה כְּהָרַשְׁבָּ"א לְגַבֵּי הָרִיבָ"שׁ. אַף שֶׁמְּקִיל, וְכַמַּהֲרַשְׁדָּ"ם אֶה"ע סִימָן פ"ג. וּכְבָר יָדוּעַ מַה שֶּׁהֵעִיד עָלָיו מָרָן דְּאִם יִהְיוּ כָּל חַכְמֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל בְּכַף מֹאזְנַיִם וְהָרַשְׁבָּ"א בְּכַף שְׁנִיָּה מַכְרִיעַ הוּא אֶת כֻּלָּם. וְכַ"כ מוֹהֲרַשְׁדָּ"ם בְּחָ"מ סִימָן מ"ג דְּרַבֵּינוּ הַגָּדוֹל מוֹהֲרַ"י טַאיטַ"צַק כַּמָּה פְּעָמִים כַּאֲשֶׁר הָיָה רוֹאֶה בַּדִּין אֶחָד דַּעַת הָרַשְׁבָּ"א כִּמְעַט הָיָה שׁוֹקֵל וּמַכְרִיעַ אוֹתוֹ נֶגֶד כָּל חַכְמֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל. — Cited in the responsa tradition on Kaporos  13

And yet, on this one issue, the entire Ashkenaz world followed the Maharil and the Rema, against the combined weight of the Rashba, the Ramban, the Rashbatz, and the Bais Yosef. Nowhere in halachic literature is the tension between minhag and psak so raw. The Rema's phrase מִנְהַג וָתִיקִין was doing enormous work.

VII. A White Chicken and a Silver Coin — Later Refinements

Once the custom was preserved, the acharonim began negotiating its edges. The Magen Avraham and the Mishnah Berurah (OC 605) permit and even prefer using a white chicken — based on Yeshayahu 1:18, אִם יִהְיוּ חֲטָאֵיכֶם כַּשָּׁנִים כַּשֶּׁלֶג יַלְבִּינוּ — but only if the chicken presented for slaughter happens to be white, not if one specifically sought it out.14 The Aruch HaShulchan and the Chayei Adam both note that where a chicken is unavailable or the shechitah cannot be safely done, one should redeem the ceremony with money given to tzedakah — a coin passed over the head, later given to the poor.15

The Mordechai in Yoma (siman 723) records that the custom is to use two chickens for a boy — one for the newborn boy and one for the mother — and to distribute the innards to the birds on the roof, on the theory that just as the birds have compassion on their young, may Hashem have compassion on us.16

VIII. The Modern Reservation — When Shechitah Itself Is at Risk

The most recent and pointed reservation is not halachic but practical. The last excerpt on the source sheet, in the voice of a contemporary posek, describes what the shochet actually faces on erev Yom Kippur: the hidachak v'halachatz, the crush of hundreds of chickens, the shochtim not paying attention mipnei ha'ayeifus v'ha'tircha — from exhaustion and haste — the many treifos that pass out of their hands, and the impossibility of the knife being properly checked.17

ה' וְכָל זֶה בְּעֶצֶם הַדָּבָר. אֲבָל בַּעֲוֹנוֹתֵינוּ הָרַבִּים אָנוּ רוֹאִים הַקִּלְקוּל בַּשְּׁחִיטָה מֵרִבּוּי הַכַּפָּרוֹת מֵהַדְּחָק וְהַלַּחַץ, וְאֵין הַשּׁוֹחֲטִים מַשְׁגִּיחִים מִפְּנֵי הָעֲיֵפוּת וְהַטִּרְחָה, וְכַמָּה טְרֵפוֹת יָצְאוּ מִתַּחַת יָדָם, וְגַם הַסַּכִּין אִי אֶפְשָׁר לִהְיוֹת בָּדוּק כָּרָאוּי, וְגַם עַל יְדֵי הַשּׁוֹחֵט כְּבֵדוּת, וְעַתָּה בַּזְּמַן הַזֶּה מִצְוָה וְחוֹבָה לְהַמְעִיטָן וְלֹא לִיכָּנֵס בְּיוֹם הַקָּדוֹשׁ בַּחֲשַׁשׁ שְׁחִיטָה, וּכְבָר טָרְחוּ גַּם בְּדוֹרוֹת שֶׁלְּפָנֵינוּ לְבַטְּלָן וְלֹא עָלְתָה בְיָדָן, כִּי הֶהָמוֹן דָּבוּק בָּזֶה כְּמוֹ עַל מִצְווֹת אֶתְרוֹג וְעוֹד יוֹתֵר, וּלְכָל הַפָּחוֹת לִרְאוֹת לְהַתְחִיל בִּשְׁחִיטָתָן אֵיזֶה יָמִים מְקוּדָם, וְלִמְנוֹעַ הָאַשְׁמוּרֶת שֶׁיְּדֵי הַשּׁוֹחֵט כְּבֵדוֹת אָז. — Recent posek, summarized in the source sheet  17

The recommendation is characteristically balanced: reduce the volume, begin the shechitah several days early, and where necessary use ma'os l'kapparah instead. The Rashba, who tried to abolish the custom on halachic-theological grounds, is joined here by a very different voice — one that would preserve the custom in principle but constrain it in practice, precisely to protect the sanctity of the ma'aseh at its center.

IX. Coda — A Rooster Named Gever

The Kaporos ceremony has traveled a long road. It was born in the Geonic academies of Bavel, condemned in the yeshivos of Barcelona, ignored by the Bais Yosef, restored by the Maharil, universalized by the Rema, refined by the Mishnah Berurah, and constrained by the contemporary poskim watching the erev Yom Kippur shechitah floor. Every step of the way, it has been the same question: can a man's fate really be transferred to a bird?

The Aruch's answer — that a gever replaces a gever — is not a chemistry lesson. It is a moral one. When a Yid on erev Yom Kippur takes a chicken in his hand and says זֶה חֲלִיפָתִי, זֶה תְּמוּרָתִי, זֶה כַּפָּרָתִי, he is not performing magic. He is watching, in miniature, what his own body would look like if strict din prevailed. And that watching, more than any pesukim, more than any piyut, is what makes him a ba'al teshuvah by the time he walks into shul for Kol Nidrei.

1. תְּשׁוּבַת הַגְּאוֹנִים, quoted in the Bais Yosef Orach Chaim 605. The teshuvah is preserved in Otzar HaGeonim, Yoma 88, and in Sha'arei Teshuva siman 299. The Geonic responsum is remarkable for its candor: we ourselves do not know why we do this. The Bais Yosef reads the responsum as evidence that the custom predates any known halachic literature and had already become settled minhag by the eighth century.
2. יוֹמָא כ: — "מַאי קָרָא גֶּבֶר? אָמַר רַב שִׁילָא קָרָא תַּרְנְגוֹלָא." The Gemara identifies the word gever in the Torah with the crowing of a rooster (see also Berachos 60b's berachah ha'nosen la'sechvi vinah l'havchin bein yom u'vein laylah, where sechvi = rooster). Sefaria ref: Yoma.20b.13.
3. תְּשׁוּבוֹת הָרַשְׁבָּ"א, סִימָן שצ"ה. The Rashba's teshuvah is one of the most polemical in the corpus. He describes the practice in Barcelona, associates it with the additional customs of hanging the head over the door with garlic (שׁוּמִים) and other hevlim, and frames the entire complex as דַּרְכֵי הָאֱמוֹרִי. The Rashba's ruling was accepted throughout Sefarad for centuries. Cf. Teshuvos HaRashba HaChadashos 15.
4. רַמְבַּ"ן, תּוֹרַת הָאָדָם, sha'ar ha'sof; and תַּשְׁבַּ"ץ קָטָן סִימָן קכ"ה. The Ramban joins the Rashba in calling for the abolition of the custom, on the same darchei ha'emori grounds. The Rashbatz (Rav Shimon ben Tzemach Duran, Algiers) preserves the same objection in Tashbatz Katan and also in Tashbatz Chelek 3, siman 273. The Beis Yosef reads all three Sephardic authorities as one voice.
5. בֵּית יוֹסֵף, אוֹרַח חַיִּים תר"ה, and the Shulchan Aruch there, se'if 1. The Beis Yosef in the body of the Tur summarizes: yesh limnoa haminhag. In the Shulchan Aruch he preserves the identical language. For the Sephardic communities that follow Maran, this ruling was decisive for centuries — and only in recent generations has Kaporos with a chicken become widespread among Sephardim, largely under the influence of the Ari z"l.
6. רְמָ"א, שֻׁלְחָן עָרוּךְ אוֹרַח חַיִּים תר"ה סְעִיף א. "וְכֵן נָהֲגוּ בְּכָל מְדִינוֹת אֵלּוּ, וְאֵין לְשַׁנּוֹת כִּי הוּא מִנְהַג וָתִיקִין." The phrase minhag vasikin (an "ancient pious custom") is the Rema's characteristic move — the same expression he uses to preserve simchas Torah dancing and the eating of dairy on Shavuos against Sephardic objections. Sefaria ref: Shulchan_Aruch,_Orach_Chayim.605.1.
7. מַהֲרִי"ל, הִלְכוֹת עֶרֶב יוֹם כִּפּוּר. The Maharil (Rav Yaakov Moellin, Mainz, d. 1427) is the founding text of Ashkenaz minhagim. His account of Kaporos includes the full ceremony (three swings, the pesukim from Iyov 33 and Tehillim 107, the transfer to the mother of a boy, and so on). The Rema's gloss in OC 605 relies almost entirely on the Maharil's authority.
8. עֲבוֹדָה זָרָה יב: and the Maharil's caveat. The Gemara in AZ 12b (also Shabbos 67a) enumerates practices that constitute darchei ha'emori, including seeking specifically white animals for cultic purposes. The Maharil rules that the concern applies only to seeking out a white chicken — if one is presented incidentally, there is no issur. This has become the standard Ashkenaz ruling.
9. Defense of the custom cited in the Bais Yosef OC 605 — that a chicken is chosen precisely because it is eino ra'ui l'korban. The defender (some attribute the sevara to the Mordechai, others to a comment of the Rosh) argues that using an animal invalid as a korban is itself the guarantee that no one will confuse the ceremony with sacrifice. This inverts the Rashba's objection: what the Rashba read as a proof of confusion (k'mo shochet kadashim ba'chutz) becomes, on this reading, the very safeguard against confusion.
10. The formula זֶה חֲלִיפָתִי זֶה תְּמוּרָתִי זֶה כַּפָּרָתִי — recorded in the Machzor and in the Maharil. The three-word triplet chalifasi, temurasi, kapparasi deliberately echoes the language of Vayikra 27:10 regarding temurah: לֹא יַחֲלִיפֶנּוּ וְלֹא יָמִיר אֹתוֹ. The Kaporos formula is a deliberate mirror — a permissive "temurah" outside the beis ha'mikdash. See Ramban Vayikra 1:9 for the general theology of substitution.
11. עָרוּךְ עֵרֶךְ גֶּבֶר, and the parallel story of Rabbi Akiva in Berachos 60b. The Aruch (Rav Nassan of Rome, 11th c.) preserves the story of R' Akiva who was denied lodging (ushpiza) in a certain city; that night his donkey was eaten by a lion, his rooster was eaten by a cat, and his candle was blown out by the wind — and by the morning the city had been captured by a Roman legion. Every "loss" turned out to be a substitution: the donkey stood in for the body, the chicken for the nefesh, the candle for the sechel. Sefaria ref: Berakhot.60b.9.
12. The rationale of hishtachvus la'azazel — the acharonim on why the ceremony works even without avodah. Several acharonim (see Yalkut Yosef, Moadim, Kaporos 3) explain that the Kaporos ceremony parallels the send-out of the sa'ir la'azazel in the Beis HaMikdash: an act of concrete separation of sin from the body of the sinner. The animal is not korban and not avodah, but a physical marker of the transfer.
13. The Beis Yosef on the Rashba's weight, and Maharashdam Even HaEzer siman 83 and Choshen Mishpat siman 43. The famous line about the Rashba's authority — if all the chachmei Yisrael were on one side of the scale and the Rashba on the other, the Rashba would tip the scale — is preserved by the Maharashdam in the name of the Beis Yosef. It appears elsewhere in the Chida (Shem HaGedolim, s.v. Rashba) and in the Aruch HaShulchan's introduction.
14. מָגֵן אַבְרָהָם and מִשְׁנָה בְּרוּרָה, OC 605. The Magen Avraham (siman 605:2) writes that one should use a white chicken based on im yihyu chatta'eichem k'shanim ka'sheleg yalbinu (Yeshayahu 1:18), but immediately cautions that seeking out a white one is darchei ha'emori. The Mishnah Berurah (SK 3) codifies both halves of this ruling.
15. חַיֵּי אָדָם, kelal 144:4, and עָרוּךְ הַשֻּׁלְחָן OC 605. The Chayei Adam and the Aruch HaShulchan both permit substituting ma'os (a coin passed over the head and then given to tzedakah) when a chicken is unavailable or when there is concern about the quality of the shechitah. This is now the near-universal practice in many communities, following also the endorsement of Rav Ovadia Yosef (Yechaveh Da'as 2:71) and the Chazon Ish.
16. מָרְדֳּכַי, יוֹמָא סִימָן תשכ"ג. The Mordechai (Rav Mordechai ben Hillel, d. 1298) records the custom of using two chickens for a newborn boy — one for the child and one for the mother — and of distributing the innards on the rooftops to feed the birds, "so that just as the birds have compassion on their young, may Hashem have compassion on us." This custom is preserved in the Levush and in the Elya Rabbah 605.
17. Contemporary posek on קִלְקוּל הַשְּׁחִיטָה in the erev Yom Kippur crush. The concern is not new. The Chayei Adam and the Chazon Ish both alluded to it. In our generation it is voiced with particular force by Rav Chaim Kanievsky, Rav Shmuel Wosner (Shevet HaLevi 9:203), and Rav Moshe Feinstein (implicit in Igros Moshe OC 4:75), who all recommend either beginning the shechitah several days in advance or using ma'os l'kapparah. The pattern — where the practical safeguards around a ma'aseh mitzvah eventually reshape the ma'aseh itself — runs from the Aruch HaShulchan through the Mishnah Berurah into our own poskim.
