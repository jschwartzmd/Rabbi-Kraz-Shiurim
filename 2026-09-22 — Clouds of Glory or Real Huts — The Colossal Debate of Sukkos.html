סֻכּוֹת — עַנְנֵי כָּבוֹד אוֹ סֻכּוֹת מַמָּשׁ
Clouds of Glory or Real Huts
The Colossal Debate of Sukkos — and the Ba’al Shem Tov’s Sukkah
A Shiur by Rabbi Yisroel Kraz · Erev Sukkos 5787
Recorded: 11 Tishrei 5787 / September 22, 2026 · Prepared: 25 Tishrei 5787 / October 6, 2026

A few days before Sukkos, the Rav turned from lulav and esrog to the sukkah itself — and to the oldest fight about it. Is the sukkah a remembrance of the ananei ha’kavod (עַנְנֵי הַכָּבוֹד), the clouds of glory that wrapped Klal Yisrael in the desert, as Rabbi Eliezer holds, or of sukkos mamash (סֻכּוֹת מַמָּשׁ), plain huts, as Rabbi Akiva holds? Along the way: a requested story of the Ba’al Shem Tov’s sukkah and a slip of parchment from the malach Metatron; the girsa that flips in the Sifra and the Mechilta; the Bach and the Taz, father-in-law and son-in-law, arguing over what one must have in mind at the table; the Tur’s famous question — why in the middle of Tishrei? — and four answers, from the Chiddushei HaRim to the Gra; why Hashem commanded a remembrance of the clouds and not of the mon and the be’er; the halachic fallout in Rabbi Eliezer’s own rulings; and finally what Rabbi Akiva was after, as read by the Rashbam, Rabbeinu Bachya and the Kad HaKemach — and through the lens of Beis Shammai and Beis Hillel.

I. Motzei Yom Kippur — and the Ba’al Shem Tov’s Sukkah

The shiur opened, as it was bound to, with the calendar. The regular Monday-night slot had fallen on motzei Yom Kippur — the night of lech echol b’simchah lachmecha (לֵךְ אֱכֹל בְּשִׂמְחָה לַחְמֶךָ), when Klal Yisrael goes home to eat, not to learn. The Rav offered a reason of his own to add to the long list of reasons for eating before and after the fast: for anyone hoping to lose weight on Yom Kippur, the Aibershter answers that the fast was for Him — and so He arranges that the seudah before and the seudah after leave no remnant of the fast behind. No personal gain whatsoever.

The shiur was dedicated by a friend from the northeast, l’zecher nishmas a tzaddik, Reb Eliezer HaLevi — Reb Charlie to all who knew him. The Rav remembered him as a warme heilige neshamah, a man who never missed a simchah, who went to the mikvah every day for as long as he was able, and who quietly collected food for the poor and hungry in the manner of the hidden tzaddikim of every generation. He loved Chol HaMoed, and these are his days.

Then to business. There had been a request: since the topic was Sukkos, would the Rav speak about the Ba’al Shem Tov’s sukkah? The Rav admitted it had nothing to do with tonight’s topic — but a request is a request, and so it stood as number one on the sheet.

The sukkah that was “not kosher al pi din Torah”

The maaseh is recorded in Ba’al Shem Tov al HaTorah (בַּעַל שֵׁם טוֹב עַל הַתּוֹרָה), in the section on Sukkos.1

רבינו הבעל שם טוב הקדוש זכותו יגן עלינו בעת שתקע אהלו של תורה בפה מעזבוש, היה אז במחנינו רבנים גדולים מתנגדי הבעל שם טוב זכרונו לברכה, והלכו בחג הסוכות להבעש״ט, ואמרו אשר הסוכה של הבעש״ט אינה כשרה על פי דין תורה, והבעש״ט ז״ל התווכח עמהם אשר סוכתו הוא כשרה, והניח ראשו על ידו על איזה רגעים ואחר כך פתח את ידו, ונמצא בה פתקא מחתיכת קלף, והיה כתוב בו, סוכה זו של הרב ישראל בעל שם טוב כשירה נאום מט״ט שר הפנים. — Ba’al Shem Tov al HaTorah, on Sukkos (source sheet #1)  1

When the Ba’al Shem Tov set up shop in Mezhibuzh, there were great rabbanim in the other camp — misnagdim of the Ba’al Shem Tov, uneasy, to put it mildly, with what looked to them like a new path in serving Hashem. On Sukkos they came to look at his sukkah. The sukkah, as the Rav put it, was known to be a duchkedike sukkah — many question marks — and they did not merely raise a kushya; they ruled that it was pasul. The Ba’al Shem Tov argued back. This was no am ha’aretz arguing: the Rav reminded us that the notion that the Ba’al Shem Tov was a simple man was vehemently refuted by the Alter Rebbe, who repeated to the great misnagdim many pilpulim that he had heard from the Maggid, who heard them from the Ba’al Shem Tov — to prove that he was a world-class lamdan in nigleh. So the Ba’al Shem Tov held his own. But the fight grew fiery, and then he rested his head on his hand for a few moments, opened the hand — and there lay a slip of parchment, on which was written that the sukkah of Rav Yisrael Ba’al Shem Tov is kasher, ne’um Mat‘t (Metatron), sar ha’panim. “It was like a magic trick,” said the Rav, “if you’ve ever seen one.”

The parchment, the Degel, and two years with no levayos

The note passed down as an inheritance to the Ba’al Shem Tov’s grandson, the holy Rav of Sadigura — Rav Moshe Chaim Ephraim, the author of Degel Machaneh Ephraim. A word on the family, since the Rav lingered over it: the Ba’al Shem Tov had a son, Tzvi, and a daughter, Adel, who married Rav Yechiel Michel — der Deitschel. Their three children were Rav Baruch of Mezhibuzh, the Degel Machaneh Ephraim, and Feiga, the mother of Rebbe Nachman of Breslov. The Frierdiker Rebbe reports that the Alter Rebbe said there were certain things he learned from no one but Rav Yechiel Michel, over and above what he received from the Maggid, Rav Mendel Horodoker and Rav Avraham HaMalach; the Rav confessed he did not know what those things were, nor when the two would have crossed paths.2

והפתקא הזאת נשאר בירושה אצל נכד הבעל שם טוב ז״ל, הוא הרב הקדוש מסדילקוב ז״ל [בעל דגל מחנה אפרים], והיה כאשר נזדמן איזה חולה רחמנא ליצלן, והלכו לבעל הדגל ז״ל, ציוה להניח הפתקא תחת מראשותיו של החולה, וכאשר עשו כן נתרפא החולה תיכף, וכן היה שתי שנים, שהניחו הפתקא תחת הכר של כל חולה רחמנא ליצלן ונתרפא, ובכל משך השתי שנים לא נעדר שום אדם שם בעיר, ואחר כך נזדמן שהניחו פעם אחת הפתקא אצל איזה חולה ונעלם הפתקא, ואמר בעל הדגל על זה, שנתגלה לו מן השמים שאין ניחא זאת, כי הילודים למות (אבות פרק ד׳) על כן התפלל על זה שיקח מאתו בחזרה הפתקא… — Ba’al Shem Tov al HaTorah (source sheet #1, continued)  1

Whenever a choleh turned up, they went to the Degel, who ordered that the parchment be placed under the sick person’s head — and he was healed at once. For two years it went on this way. For two years, in all of Sadigura, nobody died. No levayah; the chevra kadisha, as the Rav put it, were twiddling their fingers. And then, one day, the parchment was placed by one of the sick — and vanished.

The Degel explained that it had been revealed to him from Heaven that this was not pleasing Above: ha’yilodim lamus (הַיְלוֹדִים לָמוּת) — people are born to die. A tzaddik gozer v’HaKadosh Baruch Hu mekayem (צַדִּיק גּוֹזֵר וְהַקָּדוֹשׁ בָּרוּךְ הוּא מְקַיֵּם) for an individual is one thing; to rule out death altogether, in an entire city, is to tamper with the statutes of Creation. So the Degel davened that it be taken back. The Rav said he once saw an explanation of why he davened for its removal rather than simply declining to use it: the Degel was in a quandary — if I have it, I must use it; You do not want me to use it; so the only way out is that it disappear. The author closes with a firsthand report: he heard from anshei emes that the tzaddik Rav Yoski, a grandson of the Degel, saw the parchment with his own eyes by his zeide, before it disappeared.

Rav Itzikel Hamburger and the sukkah

The Rav then added a coda from a sefer he knew as Divrei Shalom, in the name of the Sar Shalom of Belz.3 It identifies the rav who challenged the Ba’al Shem Tov’s sukkah as Rav Itzikel Hamburger — the same Rav Itzikel from the shiur about his big debate with his friend and mechutan, the Noda BiYehuda, over that dodgy get. Rav Itzikel was officially a misnaged, yet the Ba’al Shem Tov said terrific things about him — that throughout Elul and Tishrei the Shechinah rests upon him. The Sar Shalom says that Rav Itzikel came to Mezhibuzh, raised the challenge — and something amazing happened. He does not say what; but if we set this story beside the first, then Rav Itzikel would have witnessed the parchment. And his reaction, says the Sar Shalom, was: the Ba’al Shem Tov indeed has something going, but that belongs to his neshamah, and my path belongs to my neshamah — and with that he left.

II. The Sugya in Sukkah 11b — Mist, Clouds, and Two Tannaim

“So what is our discussion tonight?” It begins, said the Rav, at number four on the sheet — the gemara in Sukkah 11b. (Numbers two and three, he added, come at the end; they were placed here for reasons not to be asked.) The gemara is discussing sechach (סְכָךְ): it must be from something that grows from the ground and is not mekabel tumah (מְקַבֵּל טֻמְאָה). Mena hani milei? Where do we know this?

מנא הני מילי? אמר ריש לקיש, אמר קרא ואד יעלה מן הארץ, מה אד דבר שאינו מקבל טומאה וגידולו מן הארץ אף סוכה דבר שאין מקבל טומאה וגידולו מן הארץ. הניחא למאן דאמר ענני כבוד היו, אלא למאן דאמר סוכות ממש עשו להם מאי איכא למימר? דתניא, כי בסוכות הושבתי את בני ישראל, ענני כבוד היו דברי ר׳ אליעזר, ר״ע אומר סוכות ממש עשו להם. הניחא לר״א, אלא לר״ע מאי איכא למימר… — Sukkah 11b (source sheet #4)  4

Reish Lakish derives it from the pasuk in Bereishis, וְאֵד יַעֲלֶה מִן הָאָרֶץ — a mist rising from the ground. A mist is not a kli, hence not mekabel tumah, and it grows from the ground. The connection to sukkah? We sit in the sukkah to commemorate the clouds of glory, so the sechach, at least, must resemble them — and the clouds, in Bereishis, rise from the earth.

But the gemara immediately challenges the premise. Where did we get that the sukkah commemorates clouds? The pasuk in Emor says כִּי בַסֻּכּוֹת הוֹשַׁבְתִּי אֶת בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל — I placed you in sukkos. The Rav stressed the word: Hashem takes the credit. He does not say, “because you sat in sukkos in the desert”; He says hoshavti. And what were those sukkos? Rabbi Eliezer: ananei kavod hayu. Rabbi Akiva: sukkos mamash asu lahem. Reish Lakish’s derashah works for Rabbi Eliezer; what does Rabbi Akiva do for a source that the sechach must grow from the ground and be tahor? (The gemara goes on to answer, but the Rav’s interest was the machlokes beneath it.)

Do the two shitos really exclude each other?

The Rav pressed the obvious kushya. Some write that according to Rabbi Eliezer there were clouds but no individual huts. That cannot be: Bilaam said מַה טֹּבוּ אֹהָלֶיךָ יַעֲקֹב, and the chazal read it as the entrances of the tents not facing one another. Every Jew had a tent. Is Rabbi Eliezer going to say the camp was one big dormitory, “blankets and pillows”? Chas v’shalom. And Rabbi Akiva, for his part, surely agrees that the clouds existed — Parshas Beshalach speaks of a cloud again and again. Ela mai — both existed. The question is only what we are remembering.5

Two further wrinkles the Rav touched on. First, the Rokeach — Rav Elazar of Worms, the foremost talmid of Rabbeinu Yehudah HeChassid, writing in Germany at the end of the 1100s — quotes yesh omrim that Klal Yisrael sat in sukkos only at the end of the forty years, when the wars to enter Eretz Yisrael began.6 Second, the Mabit asks whether Klal Yisrael performed the mitzvah of sukkah at all during the forty years — a sukkah under a cloud being like a sukkah under a tree. The Rav noted that clouds by themselves are no problem (nobody asks the clouds to move aside so that he can see the stars). The problem would arise if the clouds are a chatzitzah (חֲצִיצָה), a barrier; and a more serious problem, if the clouds were real mechitzos (מְחִצּוֹת) on every side: then there would be no reshus harabim (רְשׁוּת הָרַבִּים) in the camp, and the mekoshesh could not have been liable for ma’avir arba amos. Rav Yosef Engel raises this in his Gilyonei HaShas, and a listener added that the Frierdiker Rebbe too dealt with it.7

That, in turn, touches the source of the melachah of carrying itself — Moshe’s call in Parshas Vayakhel that no more should be brought for the Mishkan.8 The Rav floated, and in the room it was debated without a clean resolution, whether the clouds had perhaps taken a break at that very moment; the Rav comes back to it in connection with the Gra, in chapter V below.

The first great question, though, is Rabbi Akiva’s. If he agrees there were clouds, why would he pour cold water on the miracle? Rabbi Eliezer is his own rebbe — Rabbi Akiva had a few, Rav Tarfon, Nachum Ish Gamzu, and of course Rabbi Eliezer and Rabbi Yehoshua — and Rabbi Eliezer had already said ananei kavod. Is Rabbi Akiva now saying “don’t get so excited, it’s plain old booths”? Ribbono shel Olam, what is Rabbi Akiva up to? The Rav promised the answer for the grand finale.

III. The Flipped Girsa — Sifra, Mechilta, and the Maharatz Chayes

The puzzle deepens when we open the halachic midrashim. In the Sifra — the Toras Kohanim on Vayikra, so named in the singular because it is on a single sefer, as opposed to the Sifrei on Bamidbar and Devarim — the shitos are flipped: Rabbi Eliezer says sukkos mamash, Rabbi Akiva says ananei ha’kavod. The same appears in the Mechilta on Parshas Bo, on the pasuk of Klal Yisrael traveling from Ra’amses to Sukkos (source #6).9

סוכות ממש דכתיב ויעקב נסע סוכתה דברי רבי אליעזר. וחכמים אומרים אין סוכות אלא מקום שנאמר ויסעו מסוכות ויחנו באתם מה איתם מקום אף סוכות מקום. רבי עקיבא אומר אין סוכות אלא ענני כבוד. — Mechilta, on the pasuk of Ra’amses to Sukkos (source sheet #6)  9

Three opinions as to why there is a place called Sukkos: Rabbi Eliezer — because they actually built booths there, as Yaakov did; the Chachamim — it is just the name of a place, like Eisam; Rabbi Akiva — ein sukkos ela ananei kavod. And here Rabbi Akiva’s role at least makes sense: “He is not pouring cold water — farkert, he is turning on the fire.” The Rav added a note: in the Mechilta the dispute may not be about the whole forty years but about that specific place, and it is hard to see why a mitzvah l’doros would commemorate one encampment — though the pasuk does say hoshavti … b’hotzi’i osam me’eretz Mitzrayim, which someone suggested refers to just that short episode. To the Rav it seemed strange.

The Maharatz Chayes: the Shas has it right

Number seven on the sheet is the Maharatz Chayes, Rav Tzvi Hirsch Chayes, one of the great geonim of the early 1800s, in his glosses to the gemara. He notes that Rav Yeshayah Berlin pointed out that the girsa in the Toras Kohanim is the reverse, and the Mechilta on vayis’u me’Ra’amses is likewise like the Toras Kohanim — two against one.10

שם ענני כבוד הי׳ דברי ר׳ אליעזר ר״ע אומר סוכות ממש. נ״ב הגאון ר״י ברלין התעורר דבת״כ הגירסא בהיפך דר״א ס״ל סוכות ממש ור״ע ס״ל ענני כבוד ועי׳ מכילתא פ׳ בא על קרא ויסעו מרעמסס ג״כ כגירסת ת״כ. אולם לדעתי גירסת הש״ס מדוקדקת יותר דהרי העידו חז״ל מגילה ג׳ ע״ב דאונקלוס תרגם מפי ר׳ אליעזר ור׳ יהושע וכאן תרגם אונקלוס בסוכות במטללות ענני היינו ענני כבוד, ולגירסת ת״כ ומכילתא ה״כ תרגם שלא כשיטת ר׳ אליעזר דס״ל סוכות ממש, ה״ו דגירסת הבבלי נכונה דר״א ס״ל ענני כבוד ואתא אונקלוס בשיטת רבו ר׳ אליעזר, ונכון. — Maharatz Chayes, glosses on Sukkah 11b (source sheet #7)  10

But, says the Maharatz Chayes, l’da’ati (לְדַעְתִּי), the girsa of the Shas is the more exact. The gemara in Megillah testifies that Onkelos translated mipi Rabbi Eliezer and Rabbi Yehoshua; and what does Onkelos say on Parshas Emor? Ba’sukkos — b’matlelan ananei: the shade of clouds. Onkelos, Rabbi Eliezer’s student, says ananei kavod; therefore Rabbi Eliezer said ananei kavod. If we followed the version of the Toras Kohanim and Mechilta, Onkelos would be translating against his own rebbe. Ela vaday, concludes the Maharatz Chayes, the girsa of the Bavli is correct and the Toras Kohanim and Mechilta are the ones in which the shitos were accidentally switched, somewhere in the last fifteen hundred or two thousand years of copying.

IV. What Must You Have in Mind? — the Bach and the Taz

The first practical ramification is what one must have in mind when sitting in the sukkah on the first night. On the first night there is a chovas gavra (חוֹבַת גַּבְרָא) to sit in the sukkah and to eat a kazayis of bread — just as, six months earlier or later, one eats a kazayis of matzah — and that is where we say l’ma’an yed’u doroseichem (לְמַעַן יֵדְעוּ דֹרֹתֵיכֶם). So what are you supposed to be thinking? According to Rabbi Akiva, sukkos mamash — “it just sounds so bizarre.” The Tur and the Shulchan Aruch pasken like Rabbi Eliezer: you are to have in mind the ananei ha’kavod.11

A machlokes between a father-in-law and a son-in-law

“Not me and my father-in-law,” said the Rav; “we get along; we haven’t argued yet.” The Bach (ב״ח) and the Taz (ט״ז) — and this was not their first dispute. The Rav told the well-known story: the Bach had promised his son-in-law that after the wedding he would support him with meat every day so that he could learn. The Bach fell on hard times and served him lung. The Taz said: this is not meat; and he took his father-in-law to a din Torah. The dayanim ruled that lung is fleishigs — it is meat. People asked the Taz how he could take a father-in-law in distress to beis din. His answer: because the Bach had fallen on hard times, I am not eating; I am weaker and not learning with the same gusto; and in Shamayim there is a kitrug against my father-in-law that his actions are hurting my learning. I wanted a ruling down here so that Above they would rule the same way — and then no complaint remains against him; he is keeping his side of the deal. “Farkert, I did it for the benefit of my father-in-law.” (The Rav, who has told this to the bochurim many times so that they bring it to the hanhalah, reported that the hanhalah responded with hot dogs.)

The Bach and the Taz disagree about our sugya, and the Rav gave the point of each. There is also a beautiful discussion in the Pri Megadim at the beginning of Hilchos Sukkah.12

  • The Bach: according to Rabbi Eliezer, the clouds of glory are not the point in themselves. If they were, why is there no Yom Tov for the be’er of Miriam and for the mon? The clouds are simply another instance of how Hashem took care of us when He took us out of Mitzrayim — so sukkah is zecher li’tziyas Mitzrayim. According to Rabbi Akiva, sukkos mamash, it has nothing to do with yetzias Mitzrayim; the phrase b’hotzi’i in the pasuk is only a time reference.
  • The Taz: precisely the opposite. According to Rabbi Akiva, huts alone are nothing — what is there to remember in plain huts? It must be that we are remembering the leaving of Mitzrayim, which gives the huts their significance. According to Rabbi Eliezer, the clouds of glory are sufficient in themselves to warrant a Yom Tov.

The Rav then said that the machlokes has teeth in practice. He told of the first night of Sukkos when, just before chatzos, he suddenly realized that the entire seudah had gone by without any thought of the ananei ha’kavod — so he went back to the sukkah, ate another kazayis, and had the right thing in mind. The Pri Megadim holds that if you missed the kavanah you need not go back; the Bikurei Yaakov seems to disagree, and says you would have to. But even then, what is the right thing to have in mind? Even according to Rabbi Eliezer, the Bach says yetzias Mitzrayim and the Taz says ananei ha’kavod. L’chatchilah, the Rav concluded, one should have in mind both. If you had in mind only yetzias Mitzrayim, the Bach would say you are fine; the Taz would say you missed the whole point.

V. The Tur’s Question — Why Tishrei? — and Four Answers

This leads to the famous question of the Tur at the start of siman 625: if we are remembering the sukkos of yetzias Mitzrayim, the time to do it is Nissan. Vos epes in the middle of Tishrei? The Tur’s own answer: around Pesach it gets warm, and people go outside for the pleasure of the sun and the porch. A sukkah then would look like personal convenience. (The Rav’s digression about Florida, where in the heat everyone runs from home to car to office, and where a palm tree provides no shade — which is why Devorah judged under a tomer, a date palm, so there would be no question of yichud — need not detain us.) In Tishrei, by contrast, the rains come, and going out into a sukkah is plainly for Hashem alone.13

The Tur asks his question only according to Rabbi Eliezer. The Bach explains why: only the clouds are linked to yetzias Mitzrayim; Rabbi Akiva’s huts could have been built in Tishrei, when it gets cold, so no question. The Taz explains the other way: Rabbi Akiva, who needs yetzias Mitzrayim to give weight to his huts, would answer “every six months, a Yom Tov, to remember the Exodus,” but Rabbi Eliezer’s clouds began in Nissan, so the question stands for him.

First answer — the Chiddushei HaRim: the night is da’as

Number five on the sheet is the Chiddushei HaRim, with a chassidishe answer.14

בטור מקשה למה אין אנו עושים סוכות בניסן שאז יצאו בנ״י ממצרים, וי״ל כי בסוכות נאמר ז׳ ימים תשבו בסוכות למען ידעו דורותיכם וגו׳, פי׳ שלסוכות צריכים דעת ובכל ימות השנה אנשים מלאים חטאים ואין אדם חוטא אא״כ נכנס בו רוח שטות ואין לנו דעת, אולם אחרי יוה״כ שמכפר על העוונות וסוכות הוא ראשון לחשבון עוונות ויש לנו אז דעת לכן צותה התורה לעשות אז סוכות אף שלא בזמן. — Chiddushei HaRim (source sheet #5)  14

L’ma’an yed’u — the sukkah is for da’as, wisdom and focus. All year we are full of sin, and the gemara in Sotah says that no one sins unless a ruach shtus (רוּחַ שְׁטוּת) enters him: all year our minds are clouded. After Yom Kippur, with our sins atoned, and Sukkos being rishon l’cheshbon avonos (רִאשׁוֹן לְחֶשְׁבּוֹן עֲוֺנוֹת), the first day of a new ledger, we are clean enough to have a measure of da’as. The Rav, working it through with the room, sharpened it: it is night that is the key. On the first night, by the time you daven and make Kiddush and sit down to eat, you have not yet had a chance to sin. By day — the pasuk says u’lekachtem lachem ba’yom harishon — the account is already open. The Berditchover reads rishon l’cheshbon avonos differently: until Sukkos we do teshuvah from fear, so that zedonos are treated as shegagos; on Sukkos, a time of simchah, we do teshuvah from love and our aveiros become zechuyos — and on that reading we are certainly in a state of da’as.

Second answer — Rav Shlomo Kluger: relocate

Rav Shlomo Kluger — sharp, as his name attests, the author of well over a hundred works, whom the Tzemach Tzedek held in high regard, as one sees in a number of letters — answers from the gemara in Taanis.15 Rava gave malkos to a man for living with a shiktzah, and the man died (a mishap in the beis din); the government wanted retribution, and Rava was put to a test: if he could daven for rain in the middle of the summer they would know he was a holy rabbi. It rained. Then his father appeared in a dream and told him: you troubled the Aibershter to change nature — change your place of sleep. That night Rava slept elsewhere, and in the morning his bed was pierced with knife-marks, from mazikim, forces that had wanted to get him. So too on Yom Kippur, says Rav Shlomo: we ask Hashem to forgive us and give us a good year though we do not deserve it — a much heavier demand than asking for rain in Tammuz. When you pressure Heaven, the forces of evil can try to get hold of you; you must not sleep in your own bed. So we go out and make a new home, the sukkah. It follows, said the Rav, that you must sleep in the sukkah; one who does everything in the sukkah but sleeps back in his bed has, on this reading, gained nothing.

Third answer — the Vilna Gaon: the clouds came back

Number eight is the Gra, in his commentary on Shir HaShirim (1:4) — though the chiddush predates him by centuries and, as the Rav noted, is almost always quoted in the Gra’s name.16

מה שהקשו (עי׳ טוא״ח סי׳ תרכה) למה אנחנו עושים סוכות בתשרי, כיון שהוא נגד היקף ענני כבוד היה ראוי לעשות בניסן, כי בניסן היה תחילת היקף העננים. אבל נראה, לפי שכשעשו את העגל נסתלקו העננים ואז לא חזרו עד שהתחילו לעשות המשכן, ומשה ירד ביום הכפורים, ובמחרת יום הכפורים ׳ויקהל משה׳ וצוה על מלאכת המשכן וזה היה בי״א תשרי, וכתיב והעם הביאו עוד נדבה בבקר בבקר ב׳ ימים הרי י״ג בתשרי. ובי״ד בתשרי נטלו כל חכם לב ממשה את הזהב במנין ומשקל, ובט״ו התחילו לעשות ואז חזרו ענני כבוד, ולכך אנו עושין סוכות בט״ו בתשרי. — The Gra, on Shir HaShirim 1:4 (source sheet #8)  16

The clouds in Parshas Beshalach, the Rav noted, are not the problem: Rashi there says that the cloud left at night, and that when Mitzrayim caught up it moved behind us — it sounds like the cloud that led, not the one that surrounded. But the Gra adds something fantastic: when Klal Yisrael made the eigel, the clouds left them. And they did not return until the building of the Mishkan, which was there to atone for the eigel. Moshe descended on Yom Kippur; on the day after he gathered them — vayakhel Moshe — and instructed them on the Mishkan: 11 Tishrei. They brought nedavah baboker baboker, two mornings: 12 and 13 Tishrei. On the 14th, the craftsmen took the gold from Moshe, weighed and counted it. And on the 15th they began to build — and the clouds returned. That, says the Gra, is why we make Sukkos on the 15th of Tishrei: not simply that there were clouds, but that they came back after we lost them.

The Rav said that this is not only the Gra: there are earlier sources, and the Litvishe world quotes it from the Gra because he is the one who said it so memorably. He showed three. The Pri HaAdamah (#9), one of the great Sephardic rabbanim of some three hundred years ago, quotes the Maharam Chaviv, who in turn found it in a sefer called Levanas HaSapir: b’avon ha’eigel the clouds left, and they returned in the merit of Aharon. A note on whose merit it was: the gemara in Taanis says that the clouds were in the merit of Aharon, the be’er in the merit of Miriam and the mon in the merit of Moshe; yet Bava Metzia says that all three were in the merit of Avraham, who gave his guests butter and milk, water, and v’hu omeid aleihem — he stood over them. So the Maharsha explains: in Avraham’s merit alone we would have received them only temporarily; to continue for forty years there needed to be a living tzaddik.17

עוד קשה בשלמא למאן דאמר סוכות ממש אתי שפיר דבאותו זמן היה, אבל למאן דאמר בעד ענני כבוד קשה אמאי, וכתב דמצא בספר לבנת הספיר דבעון העגל נסתלקו הענני כבוד ואחר כך חזרו בזכות אהרן, וחזרתם היה אחר שנצטוו במשכן והליווי היה מוצאי יום הכיפורים ונדבת המשכן היה ב׳ בקרים כמ״ש בבקר בבקר… ולכן ביום ט״ו שכבר נשלמו המעשים חזרו העננים… ולכן אנו עושים להודיע כחה של תשובה וכו׳. — Pri HaAdamah, quoting Maharam Chaviv and Levanas HaSapir (source sheet #9)  17

The third source is Rabbi Yehoshua ibn Shuaib, a talmid of the Rashba (#11, printed in the 1500s), who says that all the Rishonim asked: what is the reason that Hashem commanded a sukkah on the 15th of Tishrei, when we were redeemed on the 15th of Nissan? According to him it was not that the clouds had come and left — the clouds did not descend at all until after Yom Kippur: from Pesach to Atzeres they were busy with Matan Torah and its aftermath, Amalek, the meriva, the bread and the water; then after Matan Torah came the eigel, and they were nezufin, in a kind of excommunication, and the clouds did not descend until after Yom Kippur had atoned. Then came the command about the Mishkan, and in the following days they were busy with the donations: b’yom yud-daled asu sukkos u’v’yom tes-vav hischilu lashevet bahen.18

לנו לבאר הענין שאלו הראשונים ז״ל מה טעם צוה השם לעשות בצצית סוכה בט״ו בתשרי כי בט״ו בניסן נגאלו אבל בט״ו בתשרי בין למאן דאמר סוכות ממש בין למאן דאמר ענני כבוד למה היו בתשרי כי יותר היו ראוין בסיון בתמוז ובאב שהן ימי הקיץ… ובזה נוכל לומר כי מפסח שיצאו עד עצרת היו עסוקין בנתן תורה ובטהרתן וקרה להם מקרים בעמלק ובמריבה ובלחם והמים ולאחר מתן תורה עשו העגל והיו נזופין ולא ירדו ענני כבוד עד אחר יום הכיפורים שנתכפר עון העגל ולמחרתו נצטוו בהקמת המשכן והיו טרודין בהבאת הנדבה בשני בקרים דכתיב והם הביאו עוד נדבה בבקר בבקר וביום י״ד עשו סוכות ויום ט״ו התחילו לישב בהן. — R’ Yehoshua ibn Shuaib, derashos (source sheet #11)  18

Fourth answer — the kocho shel teshuvah

With this, the Pri HaAdamah brings the answer to the second question as well — which the Rav said has bothered everyone, and which will occupy the next chapter. We celebrate the clouds, and not the be’er and the mon, because the clouds were lost through sin and returned through teshuvah. The be’er and the mon did not leave us because of sin — Miriam’s be’er was lost only later, on her death. And so, says the Maharam Chaviv, lachen anu osim l’hodia kocha shel teshuvah — we make this Yom Tov to proclaim the power of teshuvah. “L’chaim, l’chaim v’livrachah,” said the Rav: the message of Sukkos is the power of repentance.

The Rav added one remark on the timing, with the Zohar: there is an opinion in the Zohar that Moshe gathered the people and told them of the Mishkan even before the eigel, a point on which the Rebbe has a beautiful sichah. But the standard line throughout Chazal is as Rashi says: the instruction came the day after Yom Kippur.19

Back to the earlier question of the mechitzos: on the Gra’s chronology the clouds had not yet returned on the day Moshe called out that no more should be brought, so the Rav suggested that at that moment the question of mechitzos does not arise for the source of the melachah of carrying. The room pressed the point, and it was left without a clean resolution.

The sheet also supplies, at #12, a midrash from the Pesikta in which Rabbi Eliezer asks why we make sukkos after Yom Kippur — shema yigaleh dineinu b’Yom Kippurim l’galus, perhaps our verdict on Yom Kippur was exile, and so we leave our homes for the sukkah as a kind of exile — with comments of a sefer labelled on the sheet “Eliyah Rabbah.” The recording does not take up this excerpt explicitly; it is recorded in the Inventory below.20

VI. Why the Clouds — and Not the Mon and the Be’er?

The second great kushya, which everyone asks, is this: according to Rabbi Eliezer, why do the ananei ha’kavod merit their own Yom Tov and not the be’er and the mon? We have already met two answers — the Bach (the clouds are only an excuse to remember yetzias Mitzrayim) and the Maharam Chaviv and Levanas HaSapir (the power of teshuvah). The Rav now gave four more, drawn from the sheet.

The Bnei Yissaschar: the table is the mon and the be’er

Number thirteen is the Bnei Yissaschar of Dinov, who opens with the kadmonim’s own kushya: the gemara says that Hashem gave Klal Yisrael three good gifts in the desert — the mon, the be’er and the ananei kavod — yet commanded a remembrance only of the clouds.21 His first answer, al pi pshuto, is from the Chida in the name of Rav Tzemach David: the be’er tasted like whatever drink a person wished, just as the mon tasted like whatever food he wished. Since on Sukkos we are commanded in simchas ha’chag to eat meat, drink wine and she’ar matamim, we already have the mon in all the delicacies and the be’er in all the different drinks (“you cannot have just water or plain seltzer; you need different flavors”). So the sukkah is the clouds; the food, the mon; the drink, the be’er. But, he says, al da ein anu marvin tzimaoneinu — this does not quench our thirst.

הקדמונים אחזו שער לדרוש ולחקור על מה עשה י״י ככה, (תענית ט.) ג׳ מתנות טובות נתן הקב״ה לישראל במדבר, מן ובאר וענני כבוד, הנה צוה הש״י זכר לענני הכבוד במצות סוכה, ולא עשה כן במן ובאר, והנה נראה לי על פי פשוטו, על פי מה שכתב הרב הגדול חיד״א זללה״ה (דבש לפי ב׳ ג׳) בשם הרב צמח דוד, דגם הבאר היה בו טעם כל המשקין כפי רצון האדם, כמו שהמן היה בו טעם כל המאכלים, אם כן כיון שנצטוינו בשמחת החג בבשר ויין ושאר מטעמים, הנה יש זכר למן ובאר, הנה זהו לפי פשוטו, אבל עדיין אין זה מרווה צמאוננו. — Bnei Yissaschar (source sheet #13)  21

The Chida: a luxury, a gift

Numbers fourteen through sixteen are all from the Chida’s Rosh David, as cited by the Bnei Yissaschar.22 First (#14), in the name of Rav Chaim Kapusi — the very Rav Chaim Kapusi the Rav had mentioned in the shiur on the bribe, the man who lost his sight and had it restored:

דהמן ובאר הלא המה לחם ומים שהן הכרחיים לחיי האדם ולא הוה כל כך תוספת טובה, אבל מה שהקיפן הש״י בענני כבוד, חיבה יתירה נודעת להם בתוספת טובה, לזה צונו הש״י במצותיו לעשות זכר רק למתנה הזו, באמרו ית״ש כי בסוכות הושבתי את בני ישראל. — Chida, Rosh David, in the name of Rav Chaim Kapusi, as quoted in Bnei Yissaschar (source sheet #14)  22

The mon is food, the be’er is water — hecheirachiyim l’chaiyei adam; a person must eat and drink to live. That Hashem took us into the desert obliged Him to sustain us. But that He surrounded us in clouds, protecting us from sharav v’shemesh, flattening the terrain, keeping our clothes clean, so that no one had to launder or go shopping — this was not necessary at all; chiba yeseirah (חִיבָּה יְתֵרָה), a special love. This is the gift — and therefore He commanded a remembrance of the gift only. That is what hoshavti means: it came entirely from My side.

Two more from the Chida follow. The second (#15), in the name of Rav Yehudah Chaviliyo (as spelled on the sheet): the clouds were only for Yisrael — the erev rav were expelled by the clouds, as the Zohar says — whereas the mon and the be’er were for everyone who joined the march. Since the clouds were unique to us, it is the clouds we remember. The third (#16), in the name of Rav Yeshua Zin: the mon and the be’er were given in response to complaints — asher bikshu, they asked for food and for water — whereas the clouds were never asked for. The Rav smiled: did they ever say “it is too hot, we need shade; it is too hard to launder; going uphill is too much”? They complained about the road, kamah lavatnu b’zeh hadereich, but never about that. It probably was not on their radar. It began from God’s part — that is something to celebrate. Not so the mon and the be’er: “we got them because we made God meshuga.” Another sheet excerpt (#17), the Maharam Chaviv quoted from a manuscript, addresses the same question from the perspective of whose merit each gift came in.23

הרב הגדול החסיד מו׳ חיד״א זללה״ה (ראש דוד שם) הטעים לנו עוד טעם מן ובאר… הנה כתב בשם הרב החסיד מו׳ יהודה חאביליו זללה״ה, להיות ענני כבוד היו רק ישראל מיוחדים בטובה כי (זח״ב קצא:) הערב רב פלט אותם, מה שאין כן מן ובאר שגם הערב רב נהנו מהם. — Chida, Rosh David, in the name of Rav Yehudah Chaviliyo, quoted in Bnei Yissaschar (source sheet #15)  22
עוד דרש הרב הגדול מו׳ חיד״א (ראש דוד שם) בשם הרב מו׳ ישועה זיי״ן זללה״ה, להיות המן ובאר ניתן להם על ידי תלונתם ותרעומתם אשר בקשו אוכל למו ומים לצמאם, מה שאין כן ענני כבוד ניתן להם מבלי שאלה מאתם רק בטובו הגדול ית״ש, על כן לא ניתן להם זכר למן ובאר רק זכר לענני כבוד. — Chida, Rosh David, in the name of Rav Yeshua Zin, quoted in Bnei Yissaschar (source sheet #16)  22

A kushya from the Maharam of Rottenburg

The Rav had a question on the Chida’s reading. Number eighteen is the famous letter of the Maharam of Rottenburg disputing Rabbeinu Tam. The topic: women on Shabbos and the three meals, and lechem mishneh. Rabbeinu Tam derives their obligation from af hen hayu b’oso hanes (אַף הֵן הָיוּ בְּאוֹתוֹ הַנֵּס) — they too were in that miracle — the mon. The Maharam disagrees.24

אבל מכל מקום לא שייך טעמא דאף הן היו אלא היכא דהמצוה באה על הנס שאירע לישראל שהיו בסכנה ונמלטו, דומיא דמגילה ובד׳ כוסות ובנר חנוכה. — Maharam of Rottenburg (source sheet #18)  24

Af hen applies only where the mitzvah came on account of a nes in which Yisrael were in sakanah and escaped — Megillah, the four cups, Chanukah. What danger was there with the mon? But, said the Rav, according to Rav Chaim Kapusi the mon was a necessity without which we could not have lived; it is exactly the clouds that were the luxury. So we must say that the Maharam would agree that without the mon we could not have survived — but af hen applies only where there is an oppressor: Mitzrayim, the Greeks, Haman. Hunger, a natural threat, does not count. “Muz men zogen azoi a zach” to make it fit. The Rav noted that this is the same area as the sugya of why women are not obligated in sukkah despite af hen, on which he had spoken previously: according to Rabbi Akiva there was no nes at all; and according to Rabbi Eliezer, what were women supposed to feel about the clouds?

VII. The Halachic Ramifications — Panim Yafos and Rav Oshaya

The machlokes between Rabbi Eliezer and Rabbi Akiva reaches into halachah. Number nineteen is the Panim Yafos — Rav Pinchas HaLevi Horowitz, the Ba’al HaHafla’ah, a talmid of the Maggid, who spent the end of his life in Frankfurt.25 On Sukkah 27b, Rabbi Eliezer holds ein yotz’in mi’sukkah l’sukkah (אֵין יוֹצְאִין מִסֻּכָּה לְסֻכָּה): one sukkah for the whole Yom Tov; you may not spend one day in one and another day in another, nor build a fresh one on Chol HaMoed. The Chachamim say you may. Why does Rabbi Eliezer hold so? Because the sukkah must be dumya d’ananei ha’kavod: the same covering for the entire journey. And Rabbi Akiva — sukkos mamash — holds that every time they traveled they packed up the hut and rebuilt it at the next camp, b’chol makom she’nasu; hence one may go from sukkah to sukkah.

The Panim Yafos then addresses the obvious objection: the clouds did leave for a time — after the death of Aharon, or, as in the Gra, for a few months — and then returned. So Rabbi Eliezer agrees that if a sukkah fell, one may rebuild it on Chol HaMoed, since even the clouds themselves left and came back. A brand-new sukkah, however, one may not.

The problem, as the Chelkas Yoav points out, is that we pasken like the Chachamim in this machlokes — you may go from sukkah to sukkah — but we pasken like Rabbi Eliezer that the sukkah is zecher l’ananei kavod. The answer: Rabbi Eliezer himself was extreme. Other tanaim agree about the clouds but not to the point of requiring one single sukkah.26

Rav Oshaya and the walls

The Rav gave a parallel. Rav Oshaya (Sukkah 7b) holds that not only the sechach must be tzilasah merubah me’chamasah (צִלָּתָהּ מְרֻבָּה מֵחַמָּתָהּ), more shade than sun, but the walls too — they may not be see-through; and, the Ra’avyah (cited in the Mordechai) adds, the walls have the same din as the sechach: they must provide more shade than sun, grow from the ground, and not be mekabel tumah. The acharonim ask what lies behind that and answer: it is Rabbi Eliezer’s position. If the sukkah is clouds, the clouds surrounded Klal Yisrael on every side, so why should the walls be different from the roof? According to Rabbi Akiva, the essence of a hut is a roof over one’s head; walls just hold up the roof — and in much of Florida, the Rav observed, you have rooms with pillars and no walls at all. That is another nafka minah.

Practically, we do not pasken like Rav Oshaya, though we pasken like Rabbi Eliezer; so one must say that one can hold like Rabbi Eliezer without going to the extreme that Rabbi Eliezer or Rav Oshaya go. The Rav added that there are Rishonim who say to be machmir l’chatchilah like Rav Oshaya, brought by the Eliyah Rabbah, though we do not act that way, as it would be very difficult. In the room it was objected that even for Rabbi Eliezer the main protection of the clouds was against the sun above — v’lo yakem sharav v’shamesh — so there is room to distinguish between roof and walls even if you hold like Rabbi Eliezer. A second question: can an amora hold like Rabbi Eliezer? Yes; amoraim are machria between tanaim all the time.

VIII. What Was Rabbi Akiva Adding? — Rashbam, Rabbeinu Bachya, Kad HaKemach

“For the grand finale — this is really what I wanted to talk about, but we will keep it condensed.” What is the pshat in Rabbi Akiva’s sukkos mamash, especially if our girsa is correct and he is the one pouring cold water on his rebbe’s clouds?

The Rashbam: don’t say kochi v’otzem yadi

Number ten, one of the best-known Rashbams in Chumash.27

למען ידעו דורותיכם [וגו׳]. פשוטו כדברי האומרים במסכת סוכה (יא:) סוכה ממש. וזה טעמו של דבר. חג הסוכות תעשה לך באספך מגרנך ומיקבך באוספך את תבואת הארץ ובתיכם מלאים כל טוב דגן ותירוש ויצהר, למען תזכרו כי בסוכות הושבתי את בני ישראל במדבר ארבעים שנה בלא ישוב ובלא נחלה, ומתוך כך תתנו הודאה למי שנתן לכם נחלה ובתיכם מלאים כל טוב, ולא תאמרו בלבבכם כחי ועצם ידי עשה לי את החיל הזה. — Rashbam, Vayikra 23:43 (source sheet #10)  27

Peshuto — the simple meaning — is sukkah mamash. Chag HaSukkos falls when you gather everything from the field and your houses are full of every good: grain, grapes, oil. You begin to think, “I am not a bad farmer; I know my way around this world; look at what I amassed.” So Hashem says: go back into the sukkah and remember forty years in a flimsy hut, b’lo yishuv uv’lo nachalah — with neither settled land nor inheritance. Then you will give hoda’ah to the One who gave you the land, and you will not say in your heart, כֹּחִי וְעֹצֶם יָדִי עָשָׂה לִי אֶת הַחַיִל הַזֶּה. The message is to remember where we came from. It was the Aibershter who gave us a home, who gave us land, and this entire successful season was His.

Rabbeinu Bachya: the ma’alah of Yisrael in the desert

Number twenty is Rabbeinu Bachya on Chumash; and the Rav added a second, in his Kad HaKemach (#2).28

So that the ma’alah of Yisrael be publicized through the mitzvah: men, women and children, in a desert where by nature no one survives, living in simple huts — as Yirmiyahu says, זָכַרְתִּי לָךְ חֶסֶד נְעוּרַיִךְ. The Aibershter wants later generations to remember the mesirus nefesh of the first. In the Kad HaKemach he adds a second layer: mitzvos have a revealed meaning and a hidden meaning, nigleh v’nistar (נִגְלֶה וְנִסְתָּר); so does sukkah.

מכלל מצות התורה שהם בדרך נגלה ונסתר הוא מצות הסוכה שיש בה נגלה ונסתר, הנגלה הוא הפשט הוא שהכתוב צוה אותנו בעשיית הסוכה רמז לזמן המדבר שהיו ישראל מטולטלין ונעים בו והיה ארץ ציה ושממה לא מקום זרע ותאנה וגפן ורמון, ולגודל מעלת הנס ההוא אמר הכתוב המוליכך במדבר הגדול והנורא נחש שרף ועקרב, והוא כלשון המוציאך מארץ מצרים. והנסתר זהו שאמר הכתוב בסכות תשבו שבעת ימים ואמרו רז״ל אלו ענני כבוד שהרי ישראל במדבר היו מקיפין אותם שבעה עננים והעננים נקראים סוכה. — Kad HaKemach, Sukkah (source sheet #2)  28

The revealed meaning: a sukkah to hint at the wandering desert years — nomads, a desolate land where nothing could be planted, and somehow we survived. The pasuk says הַמּוֹלִיכֵךְ בַּמִּדְבָּר הַגָּדֹל וְהַנּוֹרָא נָחָשׁ שָׂרָף וְעַקְרָב, in the very language of ha’motzi’acha me’eretz Mitzrayim — as if to equate the two: I took you out of Egypt, as miraculous as that was; I took you through the desert. How did we survive forty years where people do not survive overnight? That is the nes. The hidden meaning is the clouds, the seven clouds of glory.

IX. Rabbi Eliezer and Rabbi Akiva — Beis Shammai and Beis Hillel

About ten years ago the Rav began a book on the tanna Rabbi Eliezer: his story, his aggadah, his halachos, his debates with his contemporaries. A friend asked, “Who will buy a book about a random tanna?” So he refrained. Shortly after, he walked into a seforim store in Crown Heights and saw a Hebrew book, Rabbi Eliezer HaGadol, written by a shliach in Canada l’iluy nishmas Reb Dubrawsky, whose name was also Eliezer — on the Torah of the Rebbe and chassidus about Rabbi Eliezer. But the Rav has always had a major koch in Rabbi Eliezer, and there is a powerful reason for this debate: and especially in the girsa of our gemara, a reason why Rabbi Akiva came and said, forget the clouds, sukkos mamash.

Rabbi Eliezer — shamuti

The gemara in Shabbos says that Rabbi Eliezer is shamuti (שַׁמּוּתִי) — you cannot pasken like him.29 The context: Rabbi Eliezer held that even machshirei mitzvah, the preparatory acts, of milah override Shabbos — if you need a knife, bring it. The Chachamim said only the milah itself; Rabbi Akiva held that the preparatory acts do not override Shabbos. Once people forgot to bring the knife on Friday, said “Rabbi Eliezer is good enough for me,” and brought it on Shabbos — and the Chachamim were upset: how could you pasken like him? He is shamuti. What does it mean? Rashi gives two explanations. One: Rabbi Eliezer died in cherem — the story in Bava Metzia of the oven of Achnai, where he insisted it was pure and would not back down, and the Chachamim, with Rabbi Yehoshua, put him in cherem. Two: he was from Beis Shammai.

The Rav paused on a puzzle. The Ramban says that the reason Rabbi Eliezer did not back down was that the others said, “We hear your arguments and cannot answer them, but this is the tradition we have,” to which Rabbi Eliezer said, in effect, “tradition, schmadition; I am telling you what seichel demands.” That is strange: the gemara in Sukkah says that Rabbi Eliezer never said anything he did not hear from his teachers; and in Avos Rabban Yochanan ben Zakkai calls him a bor sud she’eino me’abeid tipah (בּוֹר סִיד שֶׁאֵינוֹ מְאַבֵּד טִפָּה), a plastered cistern that loses not a drop. Yet in the story that placed him in cherem, he was the one presenting his own logic against the Chachamim who quoted a tradition! The Rav brought the teshuvah of the Chavos Yair (#3, siman 94): when someone asks him a halachah l’maaseh, Rabbi Eliezer says, “I never asked my teacher; I will not pasken”; but where the Chachamim are deliberating a current question, trying to work out what the din should be, of course he states his own da’as and his reasoning.30

…לע״ד לא קשה מידי, שבמה דברים אמורים כשישאלו ממנו דבר הלכה למעשה כהנך דמסכת יומא [סו:] ומסכת סוכה [ח.], ועל זה אמרו לו תלמידיו [סוכה כח.] ׳כל דבריך׳ ר״ל בהוראה ׳אינן אלא מפי השמועה׳, משא״כ כשדן עם רבותיו או עם חבריו בהלכה כמו בתנורו של עכנאי [בבא מציעא שם] היה אומר דעתו וטעמו. — Chavos Yair, siman 94 (source sheet #3)  30

Shammai and Hillel in their temperaments

Rabbi Eliezer, then, died in cherem — and also he was from Beis Shammai, while Rabbi Akiva, b’peshitus, is from Beis Hillel (Rashi and Tosafos in Bava Metzia discuss it, but so it appears). Their temperaments also echo Shammai and Hillel. In Shabbos, Shammai chased away the one who wanted the Torah “on one foot”; Hillel was patient. Shammai was a kapdan — gevurah.31 The gemara in Taanis tells that in a drought, Rabbi Eliezer had the congregation fasting, and after the davening everyone began leaving. He screamed, “Where are you going? To dig your own graves? There is a drought; there is no food outside; we stay here until the Aibershter sends rain.” But when Rabbi Akiva went down before the amud and said Avinu Malkeinu, it poured. Yet the gemara says: do not think Rabbi Akiva is greater than Rabbi Eliezer — Rabbi Akiva was ma’avir al midosav (מַעֲבִיר עַל מִדּוֹתָיו); he never took offense. Rabbi Eliezer was not; whoever offended him was held to task. In Pesachim, Rabbi Akiva once pressed a question about shechitah — and Rabbi Eliezer replied, “Akiva, you challenge me about shechitah? You will die through shechitah.” He cursed him; and Rabbi Akiva became one of the Aseres Harugei Malchus. “You do not mess with Rabbi Eliezer.”

Heaven first, or earth?

The gemara in Chagigah (12a) records that Beis Shammai say that heaven was created first and then earth — bereishis bara Elokim es ha’shamayim v’es ha’aretz — since heaven is Hashem’s throne and the earth His footstool: you build the chair first. Beis Hillel say that earth was first, citing other pesukim: the earth is the house, and heaven merely the attic, aliyah; the main thing is the ground floor. The Pirkei d’Rabbi Eliezer says that they finally agreed, a bas kol declaring that the two were created together, and in the gemara in Chagigah the Sages hold that as a third opinion.32

The Rav brought this to chassidus. According to Beis Hillel, the essence of creation is this world; according to Beis Shammai, heaven. This parallels the debate whether a tzaddik is greater than a malach — a shiur the Rav said he still owes. Beis Hillel represents the life of a tzaddik, who pumps Elokus from above to below — chesed, water, running toward the world rather than away from it. Beis Shammai represents fire, which always rises from below to above: the movement of a ba’al teshuvah, who tasted sin and flees it — barach ha’am, like Yisrael leaving Mitzrayim; whereas for Moshiach, b’mnusah lo seileichun, not in flight. For Beis Shammai the purpose of the neshamah in this world is to test whether it will be drawn to its pleasures or tear itself away — to become a hermit in the forest; for Beis Hillel, the neshamah is to pump Elokus into this world.

This is the difference between Rabbi Eliezer and Rabbi Yehoshua. Rabbi Yehoshua was born a tzaddik: his mother would bring his cradle to the beis midrash so that only words of Torah would enter his ears. Rabbi Eliezer, by contrast — the Pirkei d’Rabbi Eliezer opens with the story — was busy working with his father until age twenty-six or so, and then fled the field and ran to Yerushalayim to learn from Rabban Yochanan ben Zakkai. His tenuah is that of Shammai: one who flees, from below upward. That is why he holds that the world was created in Tishrei and that Moshiach will come in Tishrei: Tishrei is our avodah, when we reach out toward Heaven. Rabbi Yehoshua, born a tzaddik, holds that the main thing is Nissan, when the Aibershter comes to us from above to below.33

X. Finding Hashem in the Natural — the Answer to Rabbi Akiva

Apply this to our machlokes. Rabbi Akiva is placed with Beis Hillel — patient. Rabbi Eliezer is Shammai: for him, meaning in life is found not in gashmiyus but in the heavens, in the ruchniyus. Physical huts? Where do you see Hashem in plain huts? You need an open miracle, a complete supernatural; ananei ha’kavod — that is what we celebrate. When Rabbi Akiva says sukkos mamash, what are we commemorating? As Rabbeinu Bachya said in the Kad HaKemach: when you think of it, how did Klal Yisrael survive in the desert and feel secure in such conditions? It was Hashem’s hand — but not so grand as the clouds. The very fact that the huts worked, day after day, week after week, year after year, is also godly. But you have to be misbonen in it. That is the avodah of Beis Hillel: finding Elokus in what seems natural, what seems simple.

So that is why Rabbi Akiva made such a shturem: do not think you must run to the clouds of glory. They were there, for sure. But that is not what this Yom Tov is about. If it were, we would remember the be’er and the mon and all the supernatural things. This Yom Tov teaches that the day-to-day is also the hand of Hashem. As the Ramban writes at the end of Parshas Bo: from the revealed miracles a person comes to recognize the hidden miracles.34 We think about yetzias Mitzrayim all the time — the fantastic, fabulous acts of Hashem — so that when the sun comes up in the morning, when it rains, when you are able to breathe, you see that this too is the Aibershter’s hand. The Rav saw this in Rabbeinu Bachya — and this, he said, was the statement of Rabbi Akiva. Davka after Rabbi Eliezer, he came onto the scene: do not think you have to run to the clouds of glory. This Yom Tov is far more subtle: even the day-to-day works only because the Aibershter’s hand is behind it.

The Rav closed with yasher koach and an a freilichen Sukkos.

17. פְּרִי הָאֲדָמָה (quoting the מַהֲרַ"ם חָבִיב and לְבָנַת הַסַּפִּיר); תַּעֲנִית ט ע"א; בָּבָא מְצִיעָא פו ע"ב; the מהרש"א. Reprinted as source #9. The Maharsha, per the Rav, says that in Avraham’s merit alone they would have been temporary, and a living tzaddik was needed to extend them. The Pri HaAdamah, quoting Maharam Chaviv quoting Levanas HaSapir, says the clouds left because of the eigel and returned in the merit of Aharon, so we make a Yom Tov l’hodia kocha shel teshuvah.
Flag: The identity and period of “the Pri HaAdamah” and of the Levanas HaSapir (“from way back”) are as the Rav described them; no author or date was given. The Maharsha’s location (on Bava Metzia 86b or Taanis 9a) was not specified. Left unverified.
