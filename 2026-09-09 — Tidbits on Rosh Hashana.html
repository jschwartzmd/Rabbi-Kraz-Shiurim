רְסִיסֵי טַל לְרֹאשׁ הַשָּׁנָה
Tidbits on Rosh Hashana
A Shiur by Rabbi Yisroel Kraz · Erev Rosh HaShana 5787
Recorded: Elul 5786 · Prepared: 27 Elul 5786 / September 9, 2026

A compilation of ten short vignettes drawn from across the halachic and hashkafic literature of Rosh HaShana — from the hataras nedarim of Erev Rosh HaShana, through the Arizal's bechi shel simchah, the Rebbe of Lubavitch's cryptic New Year letter, the mystery of Rivka's absence from the list of the akaros nifkados, the halachos of apple-and-honey and the seven species, the meaning of l'arbev haSatan, the shofar as the ka'las lev, and finally the celebrated story of the Berditchever and the simple ba'al tokea. Each vignette stands on its own, but together they trace the day's peculiar geometry — part awe, part sweetness, part tza'akas halev.

I. The Nedarim We Never Meant to Take

The opening excerpt on the source sheet is a passage from an early acharon (marked by our editor as רֵבִינוּ) discussing the sweep of hataras nedarim that Klal Yisrael performs on Erev Rosh HaShana. The concern the author raises is subtle: a person often utters a phrase — bli neder notwithstanding — that is technically a neder, whether by way of hasaga or by way of a public commitment. Even a minhag tov that a person has established without ever intending to bind himself can, in the language of the poskim, become a neder.1

The passage goes so far as to invoke Yaakov Avinu's own neder at Beis-El — "וְיִדַּר יַעֲקֹב נֶדֶר" — as the paradigm case of a promise whose fulfillment was delayed and whose consequences reached into future generations. The custom of standing before a beis din on Erev Rosh HaShana and formally undoing every prior neder is, in this reading, not a technicality: it is the essential precondition for entering the day of judgment with a clean ledger.1

II. The Arizal — Eating, and the Weeping of Simchah

The second excerpt places two claims side by side. The gemara and the Rema rule that Rosh HaShana is a Yom Tov, and one is obligated to eat, drink, and rejoice.2 And yet the Arizal, as preserved in the Kaf HaChaim, testifies that Rosh HaShana is precisely the day on which one is to weep. How can both be true?

הֲלֹא רֹאשׁ הַשָּׁנָה הוּא יוֹם טוֹב, וּבַמִּשְׁנָה יוֹם טוֹב שֶׁל רֹאשׁ הַשָּׁנָה, וְהוּא יוֹם שִׂמְחָה, עַל כָּךְ אוֹמְרִים שֶׁהֶחֱיָינוּ. וּמַ"שׁ הָאֲרִיזַ"ל לִבְכּוֹת — הוּא עֲבוּר גָּלוּת שְׁכִינָה וכו', וּכְדֵי שֶׁיְּדוּנֵנוּ הַשֵּׁ"י לְטוֹבָה. וְהוּא שֶׁכָּתַב לְכוּ אִכְלוּ מַשְׁמַנִּים וְגוֹ' וְאַל תֵּעָצְבוּ כִּי חֶדְוַת ה' הִיא מָעֻזְּכֶם, וְהַבְּכִיָּה שֶׁבּוֹכִין הוּא בְּכִיָּה שֶׁל שִׂמְחָה עַל שֶׁהֶחֱיָינוּ וְהִגִּיעָנוּ. — Source sheet #2, in the name of the Arizal  2

The resolution the author offers — almost paradoxical — is that the tears of Rosh HaShana are themselves bechi shel simchah. The weeping is not for our own smallness, but for the exile of the Shechinah — and it is precisely by weeping over her exile that we merit to be judged, ourselves, for the good.3

III. Kavod Av — And When It Cannot Be Waived

The third excerpt (Siman ה, "כְּבוֹד אָב וָאֵם") opens with a striking Rashba, quoted by the Rivash in Siman ר"ך: if a father is mochel on his own kavod, does his mechila help? The Rashba's answer — and the anguish that stands behind it — is that the honor of the father is not truly the father's private property to waive, because it is bound up in the honor of the King. "עֲבָדִים דְּעָבְדִין קָלָנָא לְשְׁכִינְתָּא" — when servants degrade the Shechinah, the fact that the King has, in some sense, condescended to permit it does not erase the disgrace.4

The chiddush the acharonim draw from this is heavy: on Rosh HaShana, when Klal Yisrael comes to plead for a good year, the sins that stand hardest against them are not always the ones the individual felt he had "made peace" with — they are the ones in which the kavod of the King was violated. Even mechila from Above cannot fully erase them. This is the meaning of chaita Yisrael b'chataim she'hem zilzul, chas v'shalom, k'hanahu d'perek chelek.4

IV. Aharon's חֵן and the Gematria of בְּזֹאת יָבֹא

The fourth vignette (Siman קטז) is a numerological drasha on the pasuk that opens the parasha of Yom Kippur avodah: "בְּזֹאת יָבֹא אַהֲרֹן אֶל הַקֹּדֶשׁ." The gematria of בְּזֹאת is 410; add the six letters of the word (im ha'kollel) and one arrives at the tally used by the acharonim to open the drush.5

The point of the drasha, invoking a passage from Megillah in the name of Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi and Kamtzuri, is that Aharon HaKohen entered the Kodesh b'zos — through the accumulated zechusim whose numerical weight equals 68, the gematria of חֵן. Aharon's whole avoda — both the Yom Kippur avoda and, by extension, the Rosh HaShana tekiyos his descendants would blow — drew its efficacy not from raw merit but from the chein that had been built up in the beis-din shel ma'alah on his behalf.5

V. A Rebbe's Rosh HaShana Letter

The fifth excerpt is not a halachic sugya but a michtav — a personal Rosh HaShana letter of blessing, opening with the classic salutation:

בְּנֵי אֲהוּבַי חֲבִיבַי וְיַקִּירַי מְנַחֵם מֶענְדֶּעל שֶׁיִּחְיֶה לְאוֹרֶךְ יָמִים טוֹבִים. וְהִנְנִי מְבָרֵךְ מֵעֻמְקָא דְלִבִּי וְנַפְשִׁי יַחַד, בַּחֲתִימָה וּגְמַר חֲתִימָה טוֹבָה וּמְאֻשָּׁרָה, אוֹתְךָ אֶת זוּגָתְךָ תִּחְיֶה יַחַד, כִּי יְהִי לָכֶם שְׁנַת חַיִּים וְשָׁלוֹם, שְׁנַת טוֹבָה וּמְתוּקָה וְכָל טוּב בְּגַשְׁמִיּוּת וְרוּחָנִיּוּת, שָׁנָה שֶׁבָּהּ תִּפָּקְדוּ בְּבֵן זָכָר, זַרְעָא חַיָּיא וְקַיָּימָא. — Source sheet #5, opening of a Rosh HaShana letter  6

The body of the letter turns immediately to a piece of Arizal Torah: the inyan of Rosh HaShana is bound up in the fact that on this day, three people were nifkedu b'zera — Sarah, Rachel, and Chana. And the difference between their demuyos is precisely a difference in shiur ha'amos and komah. The letter goes on to link the tikkun olam that is founded on Rosh HaShana to the very first act of Creation — that the world was k'sivat to be founded so that a mother might, at last, be simchas ha'banim.6

VI. Apple in Honey — A Halachic Puzzle

The dip, the bracha, and the tafel

Excerpts 6, 8, 9, 11, 12, and 15 all revolve around the halachos of the Rosh HaShana simanim — and specifically, the halachic status of dipping the apple into honey.7 The classical question: when one eats an apple dipped in honey, over which item does one make the beracha, and does the honey patur the apple as tafel?

The Bais Yosef (in the name of the Mordechai) and later the Levush (Orach Chaim תקפ"ג, seif ב) rule that in Ashkenaz there was a minhag to dip the prusas ha'motzi in honey; the apple-in-honey minhag was a later addition. Some hold one should eat a tapuach masok b'dvash and be yotzei everything; the Levush warns, however, that where two customs conflict, "אֲבָל לַעֲשׂוֹת כְּדִבְרֵי שְׁנֵיהֶם הֲוֵי כְּכְסִיל בַּחֹשֶׁךְ."8

וּבֶאֱמֶת הַלְּבוּשׁ בְּאוֹ"ח סִימָן תקפ"ג [ס"ב] כָּתַב וְזֶה לְשׁוֹנוֹ, וּמִנְהַג אַשְׁכְּנַז מַטְבִּילִין פְּרוּסַת הַמּוֹצִיא בִּדְבַשׁ, וְיֵשׁ נוֹהֲגִין לֶאֱכוֹל תַּפּוּחַ מָתוֹק בִּדְבָשׁ עַכַּ"ל, מַשְׁמַע דְּבַחֲדָא מִנַּיְיהוּ סַגִּי, אֲבָל לַעֲשׂוֹת כְּדִבְרֵי שְׁנֵיהֶם הֲוֵי כְּכְסִיל בַּחֹשֶׁךְ ח"ו. — Source sheet #15, quoting Levush OC 583:2  8

Seven species and the priority of the bracha

The two short excerpts at the head of page 2 (#11 and #12) codify the general rule: when several fruits stand before a person and one of them is from the shiv'as ha'minim, that one takes precedence for the bracha — even when it is only chatzi and the others are shleimim, and even when the others are more numerous or more beloved.9 On the Rosh HaShana seder, therefore, the order of the simanim is not free: it follows the halachic order of the shivas ha'minim.

VII. Why Rivka Is Not Named Among the עֲקָרוֹת נִפְקָדוֹת

The seventh excerpt raises the question of the pekidas ha'imahos: Chazal tell us that Sarah, Rachel, and Chana were nifkedu on Rosh HaShana — three akaros who conceived on the day of judgment. The Ba'al HaTurim and the Rokeach ask: why is Rivka missing from the list?10

וְאֶפְשָׁר שֶׁבָּא לְרַבּוֹת שְׁתֵּי צַדְקָנִיּוֹת כְּמוֹתָהּ, רָחֵל וְחַנָּה, שֶׁנִּפְקְדוּ גַם הֵן בְּרֹאשׁ הַשָּׁנָה. וְהַתֵּימָא מֵרִבְקָה: לָמָּה לֹא יְחַסֵּר פְּקִידָתָהּ לְיוֹם הַזֶּה כִּי עֲקָרָה הִיא… פְּקִידַת הַצַּדְקָנִיּוֹת הָאֵלּוּ זוֹ דוֹמָה לְזוֹ… אֲבָל רִבְקָה שֶׁהָיָה יִצְחָק צָרִיךְ גַּם הוּא בְּיוֹתֵר לְאוֹתָהּ פְּקִידָה נִתְאַמֵּץ עִמָּהּ בִּתְפִלָּה וְיֵעָתֵר לוֹ בְּאוֹתָהּ שָׁעָה שֶׁבִּקֵּשׁ רַחֲמִים עָלֶיהָ… רִבְקָה עַל טוֹב יָחִיד שִׁלְדָּהּ אֶת שְׁמָה יִזְכֹּר, וְהוּא מִדַּת הָרַחֲמִים בְּיוֹדֵעַ, וְאֵין לוֹ עִם מִדַּת הַיּוֹם כְּלָל. — Source sheet #7  10

The answer — simple and beautiful — is that Sarah, Rachel, and Chana were tzaddekes bas rasha or at least tzaddekes bas beinoni: their pekida was Divine chesed exercised on them alone. Rivka was different. She was a tzaddekes bas tzaddik, and the pekida that came upon her came only because Yitzchak himself stood beside her and davened. That kind of pekida — the fruit of Yitzchak's own rachamim — belongs to the year's ordinary reservoir, not to the Rosh HaShana mishpat. And so Rivka is not listed.10

VIII. כְּדֵי לְעַרְבֵּב הַשָּׂטָן — Sitting and Standing

Excerpt #13 quotes the classic sugya in Rosh HaShana 16a–16b:

אָמַר רַבִּי יִצְחָק: לָמָּה תּוֹקְעִין בְּרֹאשׁ הַשָּׁנָה? לָמָּה תּוֹקְעִין? רַחֲמָנָא אָמַר תִּקְעוּ! אֶלָּא לָמָּה מְרִיעִין? רַחֲמָנָא אָמַר זִכְרוֹן תְּרוּעָה! אֶלָּא לָמָּה תּוֹקְעִין וּמְרִיעִין כְּשֶׁהֵן יוֹשְׁבִין וְתוֹקְעִין וּמְרִיעִין כְּשֶׁהֵן עוֹמְדִין — כְּדֵי לְעַרְבֵּב הַשָּׂטָן. — Rosh HaShana 16b  11

Rashi ad loc. (#14 on the sheet) explains: "כְּדֵי לְעַרְבֵּב — שֶׁלֹּא יַשְׂטִין, כְּשֶׁיִּשְׁמַע יִשְׂרָאֵל מְחַבְּבִין אֶת הַמִּצְוֹת מִסְתַּתְּמִין דְּבָרָיו."12 The Satan — the prosecutor before the Kisei HaKavod — is silenced not by our merits per se, but by the sound of Klal Yisrael's chibbuv mitzvah. When he hears us blow twice — once sitting, once standing — where the din requires only once, his mouth is closed by the sheer excess of our love for the mitzvah.

IX. The Shofar as קוֹל הַלֵּב

Excerpts #16 and #17 preserve a beautiful passage — probably from the Rebbe or the Sfas Emes — on the meaning of the shofar's inarticulate sound.13 The author distinguishes three ranks of tze'akah:

  1. A cry that can be articulated in letters, whether ordered or disordered — ordinary human speech under stress.
  2. A cry that cannot be reduced to letters, but is still uttered in a kol pashut — a wordless sound of the mouth.
  3. A cry so deep that even a kol pashut of the mouth cannot express it — the ka'las ha'lev that the Torah records regarding Chana, "רַק שְׂפָתֶיהָ נָּעוֹת וְקוֹלָהּ לֹא יִשָּׁמֵעַ."13
וְכַאֲשֶׁר מִתְבּוֹנֵן בְּכָל זֶה, הֲרֵי זֶה נוֹגֵעַ לְעֶצֶם נַפְשׁוֹ, וְאָז הוּא צוֹעֵק בְּצַעֲקַת הַלֵּב, שֶׁאֵינוֹ יָכוֹל לְגַלּוֹת זֶה גַּם בְּקוֹל פָּשׁוּט. וְזֶהוּ עִנְיַן הַשּׁוֹפָר, שֶׁאֵינוֹ יָכוֹל לִבָּטֵא בְּאוֹתִיּוֹת הַדִּבּוּר, וְגַם לֹא בְּקוֹל פָּשׁוּט, כִּי אִם בִּבְכִי צָעַק לִבָּם — שֶׁהֲרֵי קוֹל הַשּׁוֹפָר אֵינוֹ קוֹל הָאָדָם, כִּי אִם קוֹל הַבְּהֵמָה, וּבַהַמְדְּרֵגוֹת בְּנֶפֶשׁ הֲרֵי זֶה מוֹרֶה שֶׁהָעִנְיָן נוֹגֵעַ לוֹ בְּעֶצֶם נִשְׁמָתוֹ. — Source sheet #17  13

The shofar — a horn of a beast, sounding a sound that no human throat can produce — is the third rank made audible. It is not the mouth; it is not even the mouth's silence. It is the essem ha'nefesh, the yechidah, articulating the one syllable it possesses.14

X. The Berditchever and the Baal Tokea

The source sheet closes (#18) with the beloved story of Reb Levi Yitzchak of Berditchev and the ba'al tokea. It is worth telling in its own words:

לִפְנֵי רֹאשׁ הַשָּׁנָה חִזֵּר פַּעַם הַצַּדִּיק רַבִּי לֵוִי יִצְחָק מִבֶּרְדִּיטְשׁוֹב אַחַר בַּעַל תּוֹקֵעַ הָגוּן וְרָאוּי, שֶׁיִּתְקַע בְּבֵית מִדְרָשׁוֹ. נִכְנְסוּ אֵלָיו הַרְבֵּה בַּעֲלֵי תְּקִיעָה וְרָצוּ לִזְכּוֹת בִּזְכִיָּה זוֹ. כָּל אֶחָד מֵהֶם הֶרְאָה כֹּחוֹ בִּידִיעַת כַּוָּונוֹת וְסוֹדוֹת הַתְּקִיעָה לְפִי הַקַּבָּלָה, וְאַף אֶחָד מֵהֶם לֹא מָצָא חֵן בְּעֵינֵי הַצַּדִּיק. יוֹם אֶחָד בָּא בַּעַל תּוֹקֵעַ לְפָנָיו, וְהַצַּדִּיק שָׁאַל אוֹתוֹ: מַה הוּא מְכַוֵּון בִּשְׁעַת הַתְּקִיעוֹת? נַעֲנָה הָאִישׁ וְאָמַר: — רַבֵּנוּ! יְהוּדִי פָּשׁוּט אֲנִי, וְאֵין לִי עֵסֶק בַּנִּסְתָּרוֹת. אַרְבַּע בָּנוֹת יֵשׁ לִי שֶׁהִגִּיעוּ לְפִרְקָן, וַאֲנִי מִתְכַּוֵּון בַּתְּקִיעוֹת: רִבּוֹנוֹ שֶׁל עוֹלָם! הֲרֵינִי עוֹשֶׂה רְצוֹנְךָ, מְקַיֵּים מִצְוָתְךָ וְתוֹקֵעַ בְּשׁוֹפָר; אַף אַתָּה עֲשֵׂה רְצוֹנִי וְתַעֲזוֹר לִי לְהַשִּׂיא אֶת בְּנוֹתַי. עָמַד רַבִּי לֵוִי יִצְחָק וְאָמַר: — אַתָּה תִּתְקַע בְּבֵית מִדְרָשִׁי. — Source sheet #18, in the name of Reb Levi Yitzchak of Berditchev  15

The story is a hashkafic gloss on all nine of the vignettes that preceded it. The sod ha'tekiah is real. The Arizal's tears are real. The pekidah of the imahos is real. But the ba'al tokea who moved the Berditchever was the one who came before HaKadosh Baruch Hu with the simplest possible kavanah: Ribbono shel Olam — I am doing Yours. You do mine.

2. רְמָ"א, אוֹרַח חַיִּים תקצ"ז ס"א; the halachic obligation of eating and rejoicing on Rosh HaShana. "אוֹכְלִים וְשׁוֹתִים וּשְׂמֵחִים וְאֵין מִתְעַנִּין בְּרֹאשׁ הַשָּׁנָה." The Bais Yosef derives this from Nechemyah 8:9–10 (lechu ichlu mashmanim) — see the parallel discussion in our Sept. 7 shiur on Maggid Meisharim.
3. אֲרִיזַ"ל as cited in Kaf HaChaim OC 584:6 and 597; the passage on the sheet is a paraphrase. The Arizal's teaching that the crying of Rosh HaShana is bechi shel simchah, tied to galus haShechinah, is preserved by Rabbeinu Chaim Vital in Sha'ar HaKavanos, drush 1 of Rosh HaShana. Compare Zohar Emor 98a.
4. רִיבַ"שׁ, סִימָן ר"ך, in the name of the Rashba; and שְׁלָ"ה הַקָּדוֹשׁ on zilzul kavod. The Rivash's tshuva (siman 220) discusses whether a father's mechila fully removes the halachic weight of zilzul. The invocation of avdim d'avdin kalana l'Shechinta is drawn from Sanhedrin (perek Chelek), 90b–91a. The acharonim in the sheet's marginal note apply this specifically to the Rosh HaShana din.
5. בְּזֹאת יָבֹא אַהֲרֹן (Vayikra 16:3) — drush on the gematria of חֵן. בְּזֹאת in the pasuk carries the numeric value of 410 (ב=2, ז=7, א=1, ת=400); the drush counts the letters and the kollel to reach 68 = חֵן. The drush appears in early sifrei drush on the Yamim Nora'im — see e.g. Yalkut Reuveni Acharei Mos, and the marginal reference in the sheet to Megillah 15b (Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi and Kamtzuri on Aharon's grace before Above).
6. מִכְתַּב בְּרָכָה לְרֹאשׁ הַשָּׁנָה — אַדְמוּ"ר הָרַיַ"יץ מִלִּיוּבַּאוִיטְשׁ to his son-in-law, Menachem Mendel Schneerson. The letter (source #5), written by the sixth Lubavitcher Rebbe, weaves the Rosh HaShana blessing for zera chaya v'kayama together with the Arizal's teaching (Sha'ar HaGilgulim) that Sarah, Rachel, and Chana — the three akaros whose demuyos Chazal contrast — were all nifkedu on Rosh HaShana. The comparison of the shiur ha'amos and komah of the three is developed in the Zohar Noach and in Sha'ar HaGilgulim, hakdamah 8.
7. שֻׁלְחָן עָרוּךְ, אוֹרַח חַיִּים תקפ"ג and Rema ad loc. — the simanim of Rosh HaShana. The order of the simanim — apple in honey, tamar, karti, silka, rubya, rimon — is codified by the Rema based on Horayos 12a and Kerisos 6a (Abaye's teaching that simana milsa hi). The sheet's excerpts #6, #9, and #10 cite this halacha together with the various acharonim (Mordechai, Levush, Chayei Adam).
8. לְבוּשׁ, אוֹרַח חַיִּים תקפ"ג, סעיף ב. The Levush (Rabbi Mordechai Yaffe, Prague/Poznan, late 16th c.) rules that one either dips the prusas ha'motzi in honey (the older Ashkenaz custom) or eats a tapuach masok b'dvash, and it is a mistake — k'ksil ba'choshech — to do both. Later acharonim (Magen Avraham, Mateh Ephraim) side with the widespread minhag of doing both anyway, treating the two dips as fulfilling distinct simanim.
9. אוֹרַח חַיִּים רי"א — halachos of priority in brachos when the shivas ha'minim is involved. The excerpts #11 and #12 track the sugya in Berachos 41a–41b and the Shulchan Aruch's codification (OC 211): a fruit of the shiv'as ha'minim takes precedence even when only chatzi, and even when the other fruits are chaviv or more numerous. The one exception is chaviv where the other is more beloved to the specific person eating — but the sheet's version follows the strict ruling.
10. רָחֵל וְחַנָּה נִפְקְדוּ בְּרֹאשׁ הַשָּׁנָה — Rosh HaShana 10b–11a. "בְּרֹאשׁ הַשָּׁנָה נִפְקְדָה שָׂרָה רָחֵל וְחַנָּה." The question the sheet raises — why not Rivka — is asked by the Rokeach (siman 200) and by the Ba'al HaTurim on Bereishis 25:21. The answer given in the excerpt is that Rivka's pekida was through Yitzchak's own tefillah (vaye'ater lo Hashem) — a pekida of the private midas rachamim, not of the collective midas ha'din that governs Rosh HaShana. See also Tosafos Yeshanim ad loc. and Ran ad loc.
11. רֹאשׁ הַשָּׁנָה טז ע"ב. "כְּדֵי לְעַרְבֵּב הַשָּׂטָן." Sefaria ref: Rosh_Hashanah.16b.5. The gemara pairs this with Rabbi Yitzchak's teaching that "any year in which the shofar is not blown at the start, tragedy comes upon it at the end" — the shofar is the day's active bittul of the prosecutor.
12. רַשִׁ"י, רֹאשׁ הַשָּׁנָה טז ע"ב, ד"ה כְּדֵי לְעַרְבֵּב. "שֶׁלֹּא יַשְׂטִין, כְּשֶׁיִּשְׁמַע יִשְׂרָאֵל מְחַבְּבִין אֶת הַמִּצְוֹת מִסְתַּתְּמִין דְּבָרָיו." Compare Tosafos ad loc., who cite the Yerushalmi's more colorful reading that the Satan is literally confused by the doubled blowing, mistaking Rosh HaShana for the keitz of days.
13. שׁוֹפָר הוּא צַעֲקַת הַלֵּב — passage in the style of the Sfas Emes / a Chabad ma'amar. The three-rank distinction (letters → wordless voice → ka'las halev) is developed in Sfas Emes RH 5641, and independently in Likkutei Torah of the Alter Rebbe (Rosh HaShana), where the shofar is identified as the sound of the yechidah. The invocation of Chana's rak sfaseha na'os (Shmuel I 1:13) is standard in both traditions.
14. קוֹל הַבְּהֵמָה — לֹא קוֹל הָאָדָם. The observation that the shofar is a kol beheimah, not a kol adam, is used by the Meshech Chochmah (Emor) to explain why Rosh HaShana's mitzvah requires an instrument that bypasses human speech altogether. Compare Nefesh HaChaim sha'ar 2 perek 14 on the levels of speech and the yechidah.
15. רַבִּי לֵוִי יִצְחָק מִבֶּרְדִּיטְשׁוֹב — the story of the simple ba'al tokea, in Sippurei Chassidim, Rav S.Y. Zevin. The story is widely quoted; its most authoritative published version appears in Zevin's Sippurei Chassidim, Moadim, siman on Rosh HaShana. The punchline, "אַתָּה עֲשֵׂה רְצוֹנִי," is a paraphrase of Berachos 32a on Moshe's own tefillah — na'aseh v'nishma reciprocated from the human side.
